The conquest of Rethymno — the splitting of Crete in two

13 November 1646

One year after the taking of Chania, the fortress of Rethymno in the central part of the island also fell, and the western half of Crete came entirely under Ottoman administration. Only the capital, Candia, and the eastern tip remained in Venice's hands; the island lived for twenty-three years divided in two. The capture of Rethymno was the step that began the siege of Candia — the longest siege in history began here.

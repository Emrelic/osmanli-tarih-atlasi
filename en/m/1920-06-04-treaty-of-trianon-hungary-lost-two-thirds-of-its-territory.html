Treaty of Trianon — Hungary lost two-thirds of its territory

4 June 1920

By the Treaty of Trianon, signed three months after Horthy was made regent on 1 March 1920, Hungary suffered heavy loss of land and people: two-thirds of its 283,000 km² of land excluding Croatia and sixty percent of its population, including more than three million Hungarians, remained outside the borders, and its army was reduced to 35,000. On the map the Regency of Hungary appears with these reduced borders; Transylvania and Upper Hungary also pass to the colors of Romania and Czechoslovakia on this very day. By this treaty Bratislava, Košice, Nitra and Komárom (Upper Hungary) passed legally to Czechoslovakia; Oradea, Satu Mare, Lugoj, Alba Iulia and Brașov (Transylvania and the east of the Banat) to Romania; Subotica, Zrenjanin, Bač, Murska Sobota and Čakovec (Bačka, the west of the Banat, Prekmurje, Međimurje) to the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes.

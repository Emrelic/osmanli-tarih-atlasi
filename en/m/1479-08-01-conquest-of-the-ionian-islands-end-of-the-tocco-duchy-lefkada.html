Conquest of the Ionian islands: end of the Tocco duchy: Lefkada, Cephalonia, Zakynthos, Ithaca

1 August 1479

The Treaty of Istanbul signed with Venice in January of the same year had left Shkodër and the Albanian coast to the Ottomans; the Ionian islands, however, were under the administration not of Venice but of the Tocco family, who held the county of Cephalonia-Zakynthos, and were taken in a separate operation. According to the TDV entry on Ayamavra (Lefkada), the last duke Leonardo Tocco had at first preserved his loyalty by marrying Milica Branković, a relative of the Conqueror, but when his wife died in 1464 he offended the sultan in 1477 by marrying Francesca Marzano of the Neapolitan dynasty. Two years later the fleet under the command of Gedik Ahmed Pasha, the bey of Valona, seized Ayamavra together with Cephalonia to the south; Leonardo and Francesca fled to Italy. The Gedik Ahmed Pasha entry records that Zakynthos (Zante) was also taken in the same operation; Ithaca, which belonged to the county of Cephalonia, also came under Ottoman administration in this period. Thus the sea route extending from the mouth of the Adriatic to the Morea was brought entirely under control. Of the islands, Zakynthos would be left to Venice three years later, Cephalonia and Ithaca would be lost in 1500, and only Ayamavra would remain in Ottoman hands for two hundred years.

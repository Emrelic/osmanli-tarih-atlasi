The supremacy of the king of France was established at Lyon: Philip IV (the Fair) took Lyon, an imperial fief, by the Treaty of Vienne

1312

In Lyon, which had been a fief of the German emperor since 1032 and whose actual power was shared between the archbishop and the counts of Forez, the supremacy of King Philip IV of France was established in 1312; from this date the city is regarded as French.

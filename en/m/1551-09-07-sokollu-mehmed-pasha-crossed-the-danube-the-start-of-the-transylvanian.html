Sokollu Mehmed Pasha crossed the Danube — the start of the Transylvanian war

7 September 1551

Following the disturbances caused by the monk Martinuzzi, guardian of the king of Transylvania, the young János Zsigmond, and Transylvania's surrender of the Banat fortresses to Ferdinand, a new campaign was deemed necessary; command was given to the beylerbeyi of Rumelia, Sokollu Mehmed Pasha. Despite Martinuzzi's delaying efforts, the army crossed the Danube and took Becse, Becskerek, Csanád and Lippa; however, the siege of Timișoara was lifted on 16 November because of the cold. A new Ottoman-Habsburg war had thus been opened in Hungary.

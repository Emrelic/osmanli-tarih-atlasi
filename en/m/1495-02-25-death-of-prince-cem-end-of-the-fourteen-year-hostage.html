Death of Prince Cem — end of the fourteen-year hostage

25 February 1495

Prince Cem, handed over from the papacy to King Charles VIII of France, died at the place called Castel Capuana after setting out from Rome on 27 January 1495; the sources record that he may have been poisoned by papal circles. The death of the prince, who for fourteen years was held in the hands of the European states as a card of threat against the Ottomans, at last freed Bayezid II's hand. Indeed, the sultan thereafter declared war on Venice and led his fleet to its first great victory.

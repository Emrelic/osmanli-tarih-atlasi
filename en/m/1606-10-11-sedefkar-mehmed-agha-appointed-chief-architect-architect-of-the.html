Sedefkâr Mehmed Agha appointed chief architect — architect of the Sultanahmet Mosque

11 October 1606

Mehmed Agha, who was taken into the palace as a devshirme recruit from the town of Elbasan in Albania in 1562-63, was trained alongside Mimar Sinan and appointed chief architect of the Ottoman Empire on 11 October 1606. The TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi reports that a contemporary source describes him as 'gentle in temperament, modest, true to his word, devout and intelligent'. Mehmed Agha, who three years later would begin building the Sultanahmet Mosque on the order of Ahmed I, left a wide body of work, from repairs at the Kaaba to numerous fountains and mosques in Istanbul and Edirne. That the six minarets caused a dispute over 'equality' with the Kaaba in Mecca is a widespread popular narrative; encyclopedic sources do not confirm this and characterize the arrangement only as 'untried until then'.

Sultan Abdulaziz's visit to Egypt and the founding of Ismailia on the Isthmus of Suez

3 April 1863

Encouraged by Grand Vizier Yusuf Kamil Pasha, Sultan Abdulaziz went to Egypt on 3 April 1863 and was received with great acclaim; the aim was to strengthen the loyalty to the center of the province, which since the revolt of Kavalali Mehmed Ali Pasha had begun to take on the character of a de facto separate state. In the same year, on the isthmus, the administrative headquarters of the canal company was established on the hills of Tell el-Gisr on the shore of Lake Timsah: first the village of Qaryat al-Timsah was set up for the engineers, then the village of Qaryat al-Arab for the workers. When Khedive Ismail Pasha took a close interest in the freshwater canal and had a pavilion built for himself on this hill, the settlement came to be called Ismailia after his name. The founding of the city is dated to the year 1863; the exact day is not known.

The Union of Wallachia and Moldavia under Cuza: The Birth of Romania

5 February 1859

In the elections held after the Treaty of Paris, both principalities elected Colonel Alexandru Ioan Cuza as voivode and thus realised a de facto union. Although the Sublime Porte and Austria opposed it, the fait accompli, supported by France, had to be accepted. This union, which remained attached to the Ottomans on paper, would take the name Romania in 1862 and declare its unilateral independence in 1877; it is a turning point in the dissolution of Ottoman dominion on the Danube.

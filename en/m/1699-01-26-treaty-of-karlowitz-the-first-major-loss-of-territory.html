Treaty of Karlowitz — the first major loss of territory

26 January 1699

By Karlowitz, which ended the sixteen years of Holy League wars, Hungary and Transylvania were left to Austria, Podolia to Poland, and the Morea and the Dalmatian coasts to Venice. For the first time in Ottoman history territory on this scale was being lost at the negotiating table and permanently; the treaty is regarded as the opening document of the new era in which the empire went on the defensive against Europe, and is also a turning point in Ottoman diplomatic history. Settlements that changed hands on the same day: Kostajnica and Jasenovac (from the Ottomans to the Habsburgs; the text of the treaty leaves Castanoviz in the emperor's hands, the surrender day of Jasenovac is not in the source, the treaty day was taken as the basis) and Preveza (from Venice to the Ottomans; TDV Karlofça: under the provision signed on 24 Rajab 1110 / 26 January 1699, Venice's occupied places north of the Gulf of Lepanto were returned). The map also shows Kostajnica and Jasenovac on the Habsburg side, Uman and Vinnytsia in Right-Bank Ukraine as having passed from the Ottomans to Poland (TDV Merzifonlu Kara Mustafa Paşa; IEU Vinnytsia: Turkish rule 1672-99) and Nadin in Dalmatia as belonging to Venice (no settlement-specific surrender day in the source, the treaty day was taken as the basis; the start and end dates of Nadin's period were not verified against a source — approximate information). Vonitsa, north of the Gulf of Lepanto, is also returned from Venice to the Ottomans (TDV Karlofça); in Dalmatia Vrana (Urana) is among the places remaining with Venice (period date not verified — approximate information).

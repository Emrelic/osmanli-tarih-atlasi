The Beginning of the War with Russia (the Ottoman-Russian War of 1806-1812) and the Closing of the Straits

22 December 1806

When Selim III, who had drawn closer to France after Napoleon's victory at Austerlitz, dismissed the voivodes of Wallachia and Moldavia despite Russian pressure, Russian armies occupied the two principalities and war began. The Ottoman State closed the Straits to the Russian fleet; in response England too took Russia's side and sent a fleet to Istanbul. This war, which would last six years, interwoven with the Kabakçı Rebellion and the deposition of Selim III, would end with the loss of Bessarabia in the 1812 Treaty of Bucharest.

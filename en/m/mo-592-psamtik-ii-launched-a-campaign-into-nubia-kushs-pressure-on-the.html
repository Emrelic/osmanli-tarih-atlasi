Psamtik II launched a campaign into Nubia: Kush's pressure on the Egyptian frontier was broken

592 BC

Psamtik II's Nubian campaign is called in Herodotus 'the expedition to Ethiopia'; the Egyptian army and Greek/Carian mercenaries left Greek inscriptions at Abu Simbel. The year-3 stele at Thebes (Karnak) reports that a heavy defeat was inflicted on the kingdom of Kush, and the Kalabsha victory stele commemorates it; the campaign is regarded as having advanced as far as the Third or Fourth Cataract. According to the sources, the political center of Kush shifted later (Aspelta, c. 591 BCE) south to Meroe.

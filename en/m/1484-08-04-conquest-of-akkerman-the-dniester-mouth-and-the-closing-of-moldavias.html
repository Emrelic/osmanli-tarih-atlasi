Conquest of Akkerman: the Dniester mouth and the closing of Moldavia's Black Sea gate

4 August 1484

Twenty days after the surrender of Kilia, the army came before Akkerman at the Dniester mouth and the fortress was taken on 4 August. The city was Moldavia's busiest port; when the voivode Petru III recognized Ottoman sovereignty in 1455 in the time of the Conqueror, permission to trade had been given to the merchants of Akkerman, and now the port belonged directly to the state. Akkerman, made after the conquest a sancak attached to the beylerbeyilik of Rumelia, was annexed in 1593 to the newly established province of Özü. Thus the outer shell of Moldavia, enclosed by Khotyn from the north and Kilia and Akkerman from the south, began to come under the direct control of the Ottomans; the voivodeship remained free in its internal affairs but was left without access to the sea. On the map there is no empty region between them — the dark-toned port sancaks and the light-toned voivodeship territory touch one another; the Budjak steppe between the two was still part of the voivodeship until 1538.

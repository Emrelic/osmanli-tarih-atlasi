The Iranian occupation of Sulaymaniyya and Baghdad — the siege of Kirkuk

1821

Simultaneously with the operations in eastern Anatolia, Iranian forces launched a wide-ranging attack on the Ottoman-ruled province of northern Iraq and Baghdad. Baghdad was then governed by Kölemen Dâvud Pasha, governor since 1817; the pasha had an infantry and artillery force of 10,000 men trained by Deveaux, a former aide of Napoleon. The center of the empire had as yet no such army — troops trained in the European manner were in those years only in the hands of powerful provincial governors. What ended the war was not an Ottoman victory: Ottoman resistance and an epidemic disease that swept Azerbaijan compelled the Iranian forces to withdraw.

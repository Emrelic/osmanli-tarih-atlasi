Second Partition of Poland — loss of territory to Russia and Prussia

23 January 1793

After the reforms of the 1791 constitution alarmed Russia and Prussia, the two states partitioned Poland again by a treaty signed in Saint Petersburg on 23 January 1793: Russia took most of Lithuania-Belarus and western Ukraine (including Kamianets, Bar and Minsk), while Prussia annexed Greater Poland, including Gdansk and Poznan - 307,000 km2 in total. The Polish Sejm, under pressure, was forced to ratify the treaty the same year; this was a short-lived partition that delayed the complete annexation of Poland by two years. On this day (23 January 1793), in the Second Partition of Poland, Right-Bank Ukraine, including Berdichev, Fastov, Zhitomir, Kaniv, Konstantinov, Uman and Cherkasy, passed to Russia.

Annexation of the Saray plain: establishing the Ottoman outpost inside Bosnia

1448

After the Bosnian kings were bound to tribute in 1428-1429, the Ottomans began to settle permanently inside the kingdom. Between 1428 and 1435 the town of Hodidjed was taken; in 852 (1448) the vilayet of Hodidjed, together with the Saray plain, came entirely under Ottoman administration. This was a march territory established in the middle of the kingdom fifteen years before the conquest of Bosnia and was administered by Isa Beg, the bey of Üsküp, who bore the title of voivode — since there were still Bosnian lords subject to the Ottomans around it, the region was under dual control. The city itself was also born in this period: before 1462 Ishak Beg or his son Isa Beg founded the first Muslim quarter, in 862 (1458) the Hünkâr Mosque was built in the name of the Conqueror, and the cultivated fields of the village of Brodec were developed and the town of Saray came into being. The conquest of 1463 abolished the Kingdom of Bosnia; the Saray plain, however, had by then already been Ottoman land for fifteen years.

Treaty of Kiel — Denmark's cession of Norway to Sweden

14 January 1814

Sweden, which attacked Denmark from the south after Napoleon's defeat at Leipzig, took over Norway (excluding Iceland, the Faroe Islands and Greenland) with the Treaty of Kiel signed on 14 January 1814 — the Denmark-Norway union, which had lasted since 1380, thus formally came to an end. In return, Sweden ceded its territories in Pomerania to Denmark; Denmark also transferred Heligoland to Britain. Norway resisted the treaty by force of arms and proclaimed its own constitution, and Sweden was compelled to recognize it — the outcome was a personal union (real union) that would last until 1905. Sweden could not actually take over Norway: the treaty was not accepted in Norway and the administration stayed with Christian Frederik; the Swedish army entered Norway only on 26 July 1814.

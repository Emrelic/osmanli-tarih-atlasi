The Russian army crossed the Prut and began the occupation of the Danubian Principalities (Wallachia–Moldavia) — the road to the Crimean War

3 July 1853

When Menshikov's demands in the dispute over the holy places in Jerusalem were rejected by the Porte, Tsar Nicholas I ordered the occupation of the Danubian principalities, dependent on the Ottomans, 'as a pledge until Turkey meets our just demands.' The vanguard of the army under Prince Gorchakov crossed the Prut and twelve days later entered Bucharest; behind it the main force spread over the principalities in three columns. This occupation, carried out without a declaration of war, led to a diplomatic crisis in Europe and in October 1853 to the Ottoman declaration of war; the Russians evacuated the principalities only in the summer of 1854 under pressure from Austria.

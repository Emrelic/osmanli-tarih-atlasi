'Ibrahim the Mad', or a fluctuating illness?

1644

The TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi explicitly rejects the label 'Ibrahim the Mad' that became widespread in the twentieth century; it reports that in his own letters the sultan complains of pains, loss of appetite, weakness in his legs, a feeling of fog in his head, tightness in the chest and fainting fits. On the other hand, his consistent and reasonable answers during the process of his deposition contradict the claim of a constant instability; the article concludes that his condition followed a course that was 'not fixed but varied in severity'. In this period Kösem Sultan's responsibilities gradually increased, and the fact that Cinci Hoca, summoned to the palace, offered the sultan spiritual consolation with 'effective prayers' increased his power to interfere in politics.

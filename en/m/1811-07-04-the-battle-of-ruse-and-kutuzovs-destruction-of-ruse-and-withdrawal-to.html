The Battle of Ruse and Kutuzov's destruction of Ruse and withdrawal to the left bank of the Danube

4 July 1811

At the beginning of June the grand vizier, setting out from Shumen, attacked the Russian army before Ruse, was defeated, and withdrew to his fortified position to the south. Despite this victory, Kutuzov found it dangerous to remain in Ruse; he had the fortifications of the fortress demolished and moved all his forces to the left bank of the Danube. The grand vizier retook the evacuated Ruse. Thus the Russian hold on the city that Kamensky had taken over on 26 September 1810 after a bloody siege lasted nine months. According to the TDV record, before leaving Kutuzov ordered the fortifications around the city and the medieval fortress to be blown up; in the resulting fire, of the thirty-eight mosques in the city in 1810, twelve, and of the eight small mosques, two, were entirely destroyed. The crossing of the Danube by the Ottoman army in the autumn of the same year would result in the siege of Slobozia.

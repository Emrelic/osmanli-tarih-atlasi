Villehardouin bound the Principality of Achaea to Charles of Anjou (Viterbo)

24 May 1267

At Viterbo on 24 May 1267, with the consent of the Latin Emperor Baldwin II, William of Villehardouin, Prince of Achaea, transferred the principality to Charles of Anjou, King of Sicily; William would remain in the principality for life, and his daughter Isabella would marry Charles's son Philip. If no male heir were born, the principality would pass to the Angevin dynasty of Naples; thus the Morea would be protected from being lost to Palaiologos.

Treaty of Passarowitz — the Morea gained, Belgrade and the Banat lost

21 July 1718

The Morea had been retaken from Venice, but Austria, which entered the war, prevailed at the table with the victories it won at Petrovaradin and Belgrade. The treaty signed through the mediation of England and the Netherlands left the Morea with the Ottomans, while in return Northern Serbia together with Belgrade, the Banat and Lesser Wallachia were left to Austria; in the TDV's words 'Northern Bosnia was entirely left to Austria' — fortresses such as Bosanska Dubica and Bosanski Brod along the Sava pass to Austria on the map from this day (the exact border was drawn one by one by commissioners, the TDV does not name these fortresses); Venice, however, kept its positions on the Ionian coast such as Preveza and Kythira. By the trade capitulation signed on the same day the customs duty taken on Austrian goods was reduced from five percent to three. While the treaty registered the Ottoman State's retreat in Central Europe, the twenty-seven-year peace it opened laid the ground of the Tulip Era (1718-1730): the first Ottoman printing press (1727, İbrahim Müteferrika), translation committees, smallpox inoculation observations and a new palace-cultural life under Nevşehirli Damad İbrahim Pasha were products of these years of peace. Other settlements that changed hands on the same day in the atlas: Craiova, Râmnicu Vâlcea and Turnu Severin (Lesser Wallachia/Oltenia, from the Ottomans to Austria) and Ayamavra (Lefkada) (from the Ottomans to Venice; TDV ayamavra: in 1718 by the Treaty of Passarowitz the island was left to Venice). In Lesser Wallachia Târgu Jiu also passes to Austria; Vonitsa (TDV pasarofca-antlasmasi) and Kythira (TDV cuka-adasi: article 3 of Passarowitz) pass to Venice.

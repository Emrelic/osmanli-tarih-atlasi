The Execution of Makbul Ibrahim Pasha

15 March 1536

Ibrahim Pasha, who had served as grand vizier for thirteen years and was Süleyman the Lawgiver's closest friend and the de facto second man of the state, was strangled and executed, without any reason being given, in the palace to which he had been invited for the iftar. His attitude during the Campaign of the Two Iraqs, his use of the title 'serasker sultan', and the balances within the palace are regarded as having played a part in his death. The execution is a turning point showing that grand viziers had no absolute security in the Ottoman central administration.

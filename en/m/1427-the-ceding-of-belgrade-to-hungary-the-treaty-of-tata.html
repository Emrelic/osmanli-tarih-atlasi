The ceding of Belgrade to Hungary — the Treaty of Tata

1427

Upon the death of the Serbian despot Stefan Lazarević, under the Treaty of Tata concluded between the Hungarian king Sigismund and the heir, Đurađ Branković, the fortress of Belgrade was handed over to Hungary. The fortress, standing at the confluence of the Danube and the Sava, had until then been in the hands of the Serbian despotate and was, for the Ottomans, the lock on the gate opening to Central Europe. This transfer made Belgrade for a hundred years the harshest obstacle before the Ottoman advance: the sieges of 1440 and 1456 would fail, and the fortress would fall only in 1521.

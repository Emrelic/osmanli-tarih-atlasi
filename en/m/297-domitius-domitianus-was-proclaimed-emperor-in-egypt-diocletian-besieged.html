Domitius Domitianus was proclaimed emperor in Egypt; Diocletian besieged Thebes and then Alexandria and suppressed the revolt

297

In reaction to the tax reform and the withdrawal of Roman units to the Parthian front, Domitius Domitianus was proclaimed Augustus in Egypt in July-August 297; most of Egypt, including Alexandria, recognized him. In the autumn Diocletian suppressed the rebels at Thebes and besieged Alexandria; Domitianus died in December 297, Aurelius Achilleus continued the defense of the city, and the city surrendered, most probably in March 298, after a siege of about eight months.

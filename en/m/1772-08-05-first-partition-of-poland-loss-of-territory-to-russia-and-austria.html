First Partition of Poland — loss of territory to Russia and Austria

5 August 1772

By a treaty signed on 5 August 1772 between Russia, Prussia and Austria, about one third of the territory of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth was partitioned: Russia took part of present-day Belarus and Latvia (including Dünaburg, Polotsk and Vitebsk), Austria annexed Galicia (including Lwów and western Podolia, and Yazlovets), and Prussia took over the coastal region of Royal Prussia. The Polish Sejm ratified the treaty only on 30 September 1773, under pressure. This was the first of three partitions that would continue until 1795.

The strangling of Prince Mahmud

7 June 1603

Mehmed III, suspecting that his eldest son Prince Mahmud had designs on the throne and could depose him, had him strangled; the prince's mother and about thirty of her servants were thrown into the sea. In the midst of the Celâlî revolts and the unrest in Anatolia, the sultan had seen that the soldiery inclined toward the prince; it is recorded that the valide sultan was also displeased with the prince's mother. This execution is one of the last links leading, with the death of Mehmed III six months later, to the throne being left solely to the thirteen-year-old Ahmed I and to the effective end of the custom of fratricide.

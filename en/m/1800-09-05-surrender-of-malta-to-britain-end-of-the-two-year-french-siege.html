Surrender of Malta to Britain — end of the two-year French siege

5 September 1800

Malta, which Napoleon had seized in 1798 on his way to the Egyptian expedition, soon came under the naval blockade of the British fleet following an uprising by the island's population; after a siege of two years and starvation, the French commander General Vaubois surrendered to the British General Pigot on 5 September 1800. Malta thus passed from the French Republic to British protection and would be formally attached to the British crown by the 1814 Treaty of Paris — it became the most important British naval base in the Mediterranean.

End of the Ottoman-Venetian War and peace

14 December 1502

The war that began in 1499 and shook Venice with the losses of Sapienza, Lepanto (İnebahtı), Modon and Coron ended in peace in 1502. Venice, having lost its most important bases in the Morea, was forced to recognize Ottoman superiority in return for retaining its commercial privileges in the eastern Mediterranean. The reign of Bayezid II, regarded as cautious, had with this war raised the Ottomans for the first time to the position of a serious naval power in the Mediterranean.

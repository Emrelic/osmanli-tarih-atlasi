The killing of Osman the Young by the Janissaries (Hâile-i Osmâniye)

20 May 1622

The Janissaries and sipahis, who rose up upon rumours that he planned to leave Istanbul under the pretext of the pilgrimage and to dissolve the Janissary corps, deposed Osman II and had him strangled in the dungeons of Yedikule. According to the TDV Encyclopaedia of Islam article on 'Osman II', this event is the first case in Ottoman history of a sultan being openly killed by a military rebellion and created a deep rupture in the relationship between dynasty and army. His last hours passed as follows: when his attempt to take refuge in the mansion of the Janissary agha Ali Agha produced no result, the sultan, in the words of the TDV, 'in a pitiful state, bareheaded and in disarray', was put on a nag and taken, under heavy insults, first to the Orta Cami, where Mustafa I was, and in the afternoon of the same day to Yedikule; according to some historians, to prove his death his ear and nose were cut off and shown to the mother of Mustafa I. After the event Mustafa I was placed on the throne for the second time. The event, known as 'Hâile-i Osmâniye' (the Osman calamity), permanently upset the palace-corps balance and began the debates over legitimacy that would continue for a century.

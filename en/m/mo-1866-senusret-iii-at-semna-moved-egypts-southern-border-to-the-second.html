Senusret III at Semna moved Egypt's southern border to the Second Cataract; he forbade the passage of Nubians

1866 BC

The Semna stele of Senusret III, dated to year 8, fixes the 'southern boundary': Nubians, except those coming to Iken for trade, are forbidden to cross this line by land or by river. The king built a chain of fortresses in Lower Nubia (Buhen, Semna, Kumma, Uronarti and others) and halted Kerma's expansion to the north. This is the southernmost lasting frontier of the Middle Kingdom in Nubia.

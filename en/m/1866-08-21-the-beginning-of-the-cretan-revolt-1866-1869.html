The Beginning of the Cretan Revolt (1866-1869)

21 August 1866

The wide-ranging uprising that broke out on the island with the open support of Greece turned the demand of the Cretan Greeks for enosis (union with Greece) into an international question for the first time. The hard mountain war, lasting three years, inflicted heavy losses on the Ottoman army and aroused great repercussions in European public opinion. The revolt could be put out only in 1868, when Âlî Pasha went to the island and put into force a statute granting broad privileges.

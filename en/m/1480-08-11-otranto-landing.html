Otranto landing

11 August 1480

Ottoman forces landing on the coast of Apulia (Puglia) besieged and took the fortress of Otranto; because the city was entered by fighting, some of the notables who resisted were executed and some taken captive. Church tradition remembers the dead as the 'Martyrs of Otranto', about 800 people; the TDV attributes the exaggeration of this number to church propaganda. Only the fortress at the tip of the Salento peninsula and its narrow surroundings were held; the remaining lands of the Kingdom of Naples on the Taranto-Brindisi-Lecce line were not lost — this bridgehead opening onto Italy would last a year.

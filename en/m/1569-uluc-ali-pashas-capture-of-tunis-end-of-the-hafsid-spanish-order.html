Uluç Ali Pasha's capture of Tunis: end of the Hafsid-Spanish order

1569

When Charles V took Tunis in 1535, he had not governed the city himself but had restored the Hafsid sultan Mulay Hasan to the throne; in the TDV's words, the city of Tunis remained "under Spanish protection and under the rule of Mulay Ahmad III until the second Ottoman intervention in 1569." Uluç Ali, appointed to the beylerbeyilik of Algiers on 27 June 1568, marched overland on Tunis, defeated the Hafsid sultan at Béja and entered the city; Mulay Ahmad took refuge in the Spanish presidio of La Goulette. Thus the gulf was divided in two — the city Ottoman, the harbor fortress Spanish — and this division lasted four years.

Prince Cem brought to Rome — hostage diplomacy and 40,000 ducats a year

4 March 1489

Pope Innocent VIII, in agreement with Pierre d'Aubusson, head of the Knights of Rhodes, had Prince Cem brought to Rome (4 March 1489). Cem had taken refuge on Rhodes on 29 July 1482, and from there had passed to Savoyard lands and finally into papal custody. Bayezid II had agreed to pay the knights 40,000 ducats a year in return for their keeping his brother in detention, and to grant them the right of free trade in Ottoman territory. This payment was not a tribute but the price of keeping the legitimate rival to the throne immobilized in Europe: had Cem been free he could have raised an army in Anatolia, and moreover could have become a source of legitimacy for a Crusade. Thus a dynastic matter became for thirty years the main constraint on Ottoman foreign policy — this is the reason for Bayezid's cautious conduct in the West. With Cem's death in 1495 this constraint was lifted.

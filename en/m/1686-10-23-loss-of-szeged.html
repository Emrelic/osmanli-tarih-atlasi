Loss of Szeged

23 October 1686

Szeged, at the confluence of the Tisza and Maros rivers, was the southern gate of the Hungarian plain and held the road to the Temesvár eyalet. After Buda and Pécs fell in the same autumn, the besieged fortress surrendered quickly. The loss of three fortresses in one season was a sign that the Ottoman administrative structure in Hungary, established since 1541, had begun to collapse. The atlas also regards Zenta on the Tisza as having passed from the Ottoman to the Habsburg side on the same day (the surrender day specific to Zenta was not read in the source, the day was taken from Szeged; TDV zenta: from October 1686 fighting began between the Ottomans and the allies led by the Habsburgs — declared approximation).

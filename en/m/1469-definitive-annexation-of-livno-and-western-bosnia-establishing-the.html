Definitive annexation of Livno and western Bosnia: establishing the Venetian border

1469

The fortress on the plateau commanding the Livno plain was between 1448 and 1454 in the hands of Stjepan Vukčić Kosača, the founder of the Duchy of Herzegovina. It had been occupied briefly by Ottoman forces in the 1463 Bosnian campaign but retaken the same year; in 1466 the lord of the city was Ivaniš Vlatković. In the words of the TDV, definitive Ottoman rule most probably came in 1468-1469, and the survey of 1485 records Livno as Ottoman land. This is the closing of the western end of the conquest of Bosnia: Livno became a dangerous frontier town, neighboring Venetian territory only thirty-six kilometers away across the open plain, and kept this position until 1521-1522, when the governor of Bosnia Gazi Hüsrev Beg took the Venetian fortresses.

End of the Siege of Gaeta — collapse of the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies

13 February 1861

After the collapse that began with Garibaldi's landing in Sicily in May 1860, the Bourbon king Francis II, who fled from Naples, was besieged in his last fortress Gaeta from 3 November 1860; with the French fleet's withdrawal from the region in January 1861 the Italian naval blockade tightened and Gaeta surrendered on 13 February 1861. Francis II abandoned his throne and went into exile; the last point of resistance of the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, Civitella del Tronto, would fall only on 20 March 1861. With the collapse of the kingdom the lands in Sicily (Palermo, Messina, Syracuse, Trapani, Misilmeri) and in Campania (including Nola) were no longer under Bourbon administration.

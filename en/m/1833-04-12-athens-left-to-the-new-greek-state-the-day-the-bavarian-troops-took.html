Athens left to the new Greek state (the day the Bavarian troops took over the Acropolis)

12 April 1833

Athens, Ottoman since 1456, had changed hands several times during the Greek war of independence, and the garrison on the Acropolis had returned to Ottoman control in 1827. Because it lay north of the border drawn by the London Protocol, the city was handed over to the new kingdom and the garrison evacuated the Acropolis. The day taken as the basis on the map is the day the Bavarian troops took over the Acropolis; the actual departure of the Ottoman garrison took place a few days later (St Clair, Who Saved the Parthenon?, Open Book Publishers, 2022, ch. 13; Finlay, Journals and Letters, vol. I, p. 67). The following year Athens was declared the capital of Greece in place of Nauplia.

The Loss of Sana'a to Imam Yahya

1 April 1905

The uprising begun in 1904 by the Zaydi Imam Yahya Hamid al-Din ended in April 1905 with the fall of Sana'a at the end of a siege; the city left Ottoman administration, and hunger and epidemic broke out during the siege. The mountainous geography of Yemen, the length of the supply lines and the local legitimacy of the Zaydi imamate had made Ottoman dominion there fragile since its establishment. The city would be retaken five months later at the price of heavy losses, but the Yemen question would not be closed until 1918.

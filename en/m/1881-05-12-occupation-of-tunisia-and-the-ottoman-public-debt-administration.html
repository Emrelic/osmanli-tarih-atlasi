Occupation of Tunisia and the Ottoman Public Debt Administration

12 May 1881

Using incidents on the Algerian border as a pretext, France occupied Tunisia and took it under its protection with the Treaty of Bardo; the Ottoman presence in North Africa shrank to Tripolitania. With the Decree of Muharrem issued in the same year, the Ottoman Public Debt Administration (Düyûn-ı Umûmiyye), in which the creditors were represented, was established for the management of the bankrupt foreign debts: basic revenues such as salt, tobacco and silk were attached directly to this foreign administration, and financial sovereignty was severely limited. Other settlements lost on the same date: Kairouan, Gabès, Sfax, Djerba.

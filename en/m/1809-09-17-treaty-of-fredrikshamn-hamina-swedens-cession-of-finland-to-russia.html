Treaty of Fredrikshamn (Hamina) — Sweden's cession of Finland to Russia

17 September 1809

The Treaty of Fredrikshamn (present-day Hamina), which ended the 1808-1809 Russo-Swedish War, was signed on 17 September 1809; Sweden ceded the whole of Finland, including the Åland Islands, to Russia. Finland acquired the status of a Grand Duchy attached to the Russian Empire with broad autonomy and preserved its Lutheran faith and Swedish law. This meant the effective end of Sweden's great-power status in the Baltic. By this treaty Finland's six provinces and Västerbotten up to the Torne river (Kokkola, Joensuu, Savonlinna, Tornio, Rovaniemi, Inari and others) passed to the Grand Duchy of Finland attached to Russia.

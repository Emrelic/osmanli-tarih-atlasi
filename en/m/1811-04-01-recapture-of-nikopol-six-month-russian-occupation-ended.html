Recapture of Nikopol — six-month Russian occupation ended

1 April 1811

Russian forces that had crossed to the south bank of the Danube in the Ottoman-Russian war of 1806-1812 had taken Nikopol in October 1810 under General Kutuzov. Six months later, seeing that they could not hold the Danube line, they evacuated the city; before leaving they blew up a significant part of the fortress. The TDV's record is as follows: "In October 1810 (during the Turkish-Russian war of 1808-1812) Nikopol was taken by the forces of the famous Russian General Mikhail Kutuzov. Before leaving the city, the Russians blew up a significant part of the fortress in April 1811 and did great damage to the city." After the withdrawal of the Russian forces the Ottomans had the fortress rebuilt; the inscription dated 1813-1814 is the record of this repair.

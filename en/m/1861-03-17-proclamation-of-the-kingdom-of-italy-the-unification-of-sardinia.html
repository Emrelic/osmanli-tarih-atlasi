Proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy — the unification of Sardinia, Tuscany and southern Italy

17 March 1861

At the first session of the Italian parliament in Turin, the Kingdom of Italy was formally proclaimed on 17 March 1861, and Victor Emmanuel II, king of Sardinia-Piedmont, became the first king of united Italy. The new kingdom united with the Kingdom of Sardinia the territories of Tuscany, Emilia-Romagna, Naples and Sicily (the Two Sicilies), which had joined by plebiscite the previous year; Rome (the Papal States) and Venice (Austria) still remained outside — they would join in 1870 and 1866 respectively.

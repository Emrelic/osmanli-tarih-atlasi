Peace between Murad II and Byzantium — Byzantium again agreed to pay tribute

22 February 1424

Murad II, who had besieged Constantinople in 1422 against Byzantium for supporting the pretender Mustafa, signed a peace treaty with the Byzantine emperor on 22 February 1424. By this treaty Byzantium agreed to pay tribute to the Ottomans again and to return the places it had taken after 1402 on the coasts of the Marmara, the Aegean and the Black Sea, except the fortresses of Silivri and Terkos; the annual tribute from which it had been freed by the agreement made with Emir Süleyman in 1403 thus returned. Byzantium continued in this condition until the conquest of Constantinople in 1453.

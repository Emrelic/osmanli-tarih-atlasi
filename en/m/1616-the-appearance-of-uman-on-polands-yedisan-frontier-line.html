The appearance of Uman on Poland's Yedisan frontier line

1616

Uman first entered the records as a settlement at the southeastern tip of the Bratslav region under Polish administration. Its location lay on the northward raiding routes of the Crimean Khanate; the settling of a permanent town there shows that the empty steppe belt between the two states had begun to narrow from the north. For the next two centuries the city would remain the southernmost large Polish settlement on this line.

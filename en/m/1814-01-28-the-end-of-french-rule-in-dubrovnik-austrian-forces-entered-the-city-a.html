The end of French rule in Dubrovnik — Austrian forces entered the city, a former Ottoman tributary

28 January 1814

According to the TDV, the Republic of Dubrovnik, which had been under Ottoman protection since 1365, was ended by the French, who seized the city on 27 May 1806. In the winter of 1813–1814 the people of the city rose against the French; on 3 January 1814 the Austrian major general Milutinović, who had come to Gruž with two Croatian battalions, besieged the garrison; after four days of bombardment the French garrison sent an envoy to the general. At dawn on 28 January Austrian and British troops marched from Gruž into the city; on 31 January a provisional Austrian administration began to function in the region. The incorporation of the city into Austrian territory was finalized at the Congress of Vienna in 1815.

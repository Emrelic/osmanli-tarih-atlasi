Amenhotep II reached Napata in Nubia and erected a southern boundary stele: Egypt's southernmost point

1424 BC

On his return from the Syrian campaign (July), Amenhotep II crossed into Nubia; according to the temple inscriptions at Elephantine and Amada, he had six of the seven princes brought from Syria hung on the walls of Thebes and the seventh on the walls of Napata. According to Breasted, the king set up at or just above Napata a stele marking the southern boundary: Egypt's actual sovereignty in Nubia thus extended beyond Tombos (Third Cataract) to the region of the Fourth Cataract.

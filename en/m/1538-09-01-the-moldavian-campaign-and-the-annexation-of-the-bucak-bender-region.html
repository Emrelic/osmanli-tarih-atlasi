The Moldavian Campaign and the Annexation of the Bucak (Bender) Region

1 September 1538

In the campaign launched against Petru Rareş, the voivode of Moldavia, who did not fulfil his obligations to the Ottomans, Suceava was captured and the city of Iași was plundered by the Crimean Khan Sahib Giray. The sultan installed Stephen the Locust (Çekirge Stefan) as voivode and added the region of Bucak (Southern Bessarabia) together with Bender directly to Ottoman territory. This annexation became one of the links that turned the northwestern shores of the Black Sea into an Ottoman inland sea. Moldavia was already a voivodeship subject to the Ottomans; this campaign did not take land from a foreign state, but merely took the internal administration of Bender and Bucak from the voivode and attached it directly to the sultan; the rest of the voivodeship continued its vassal status. The same structure would be repeated at Kilia and Akkerman in 1484 and at Hotin in 1713: border fortresses direct, interior lands vassal.

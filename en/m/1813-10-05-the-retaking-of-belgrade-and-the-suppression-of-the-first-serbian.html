The Retaking of Belgrade and the Suppression of the First Serbian Uprising

5 October 1813

The Serbian rebels, deprived of Russian support by the Treaty of Bucharest, could not hold out against the joint operation of forces coming from Bosnia and Rumelia; Hurşid Ahmed Pasha entered Belgrade and the nine-year uprising was completely suppressed. Karađorđe fled to Austria, and the sancak of Smederevo was again attached directly to Ottoman administration. However, the harsh measures applied would trigger a second and lasting rebellion two years later under the leadership of Miloš Obrenović.

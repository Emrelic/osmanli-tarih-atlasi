Fall of the Bastille — the beginning of the French Revolution

14 July 1789

Amid the anger created by King Louis XVI's dismissal of the finance minister Necker (11 July 1789) and the economic crisis, the people of Paris marched on the fortress of the Bastille demanding arms and powder. In the fighting that began in the afternoon, the fortress, which had only about a hundred guards against more than three thousand insurgents, surrendered around 17:00; several officials, including the fortress commander Bernard-René de Launay, were killed, and seven prisoners were freed. The almost empty fortress, which was in any case slated for demolition, was a symbol of royal tyranny — its fall is reported to Louis XVI with the words 'No, sire, this is not a revolt, it is a revolution'. 14 July is today the national holiday of France, and the event is regarded as the symbolic beginning of the popular movement that soon spread across the whole country and of the French Revolution (1789-1799) — in the following decade the revolution would overthrow the absolute monarchy and permanently change the political map of Europe and the international balances, the Ottoman Empire included.

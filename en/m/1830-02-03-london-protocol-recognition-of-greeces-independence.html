London Protocol — recognition of Greece's independence

3 February 1830

The protocol signed in London by Britain, France and Russia, going beyond the autonomy envisaged by the Treaty of Edirne, recognized Greece as an INDEPENDENT state. Provisions: the Morea peninsula, Attica and Euboea, together with the Cyclades islands, were left to the new state; the border was drawn on the Arta-Volos line; compensation was to be paid to the Ottoman State; the form of government of the state was determined as an 'independent monarchy'. This is the FIRST independent state to break away from the empire and set the legal model of the later Balkan independences. The Cyclades islands (Naxos, Tinos, Santorini, Paros, Milos, Syros, Mykonos and others) were included in Greece, whose independence was recognized by the London Protocol.

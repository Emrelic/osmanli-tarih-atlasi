Evacuation of Mazagan: Portugal's last position in Morocco fell

11 March 1769

The garrison of 592 men could not hold out against the siege begun in early 1769 by the Alawite sultan Muhammad III b. Abdallah; the evacuation fleet that set out from Lisbon on 1 February took the last soldiers aboard on 11 March 1769, and the governor Dinis Gregório was the last to leave the city. Thus Portugal's presence in Morocco, begun with the taking of Ceuta in 1415, came to an end after three and a half centuries. Mazagan was the only position on the Atlantic coast held after the general collapse of 1541 and, at two hundred and fifty-five years, was the place Portugal held longest in Morocco.

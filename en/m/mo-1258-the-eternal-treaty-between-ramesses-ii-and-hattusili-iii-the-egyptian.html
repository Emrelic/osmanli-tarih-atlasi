The 'Eternal Treaty' between Ramesses II and Hattusili III: the Egyptian-Hittite war came to an end

1258 BC

In the 21st year of Ramesses II, Hittite envoys brought the treaty tablet to Pi-Ramesse. The treaty ended the Syrian war of about fifteen years: the two sides gave up further plans of conquest in Syria, formed a defensive alliance, and agreed to extradite fugitives and to give mutual aid. According to Breasted's summary, the border is not specified in the text; the spheres of influence of the two states remained as they stood in fact (the Kadesh-Amurru belt with Hatti, Canaan with Egypt).

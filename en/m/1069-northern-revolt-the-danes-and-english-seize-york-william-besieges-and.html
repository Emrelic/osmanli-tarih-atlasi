Northern revolt: the Danes and English seize York; William besieges and retakes the city, devastates the north

1069

After the murder of Robert of Comines at Durham, the north proclaimed Edgar Atheling, and the Danish king Sweyn sent a fleet; the Danes and English took York, which was protected by two Norman castles. William marched on York, drove the Danes to their ships, took the city by blockade and devastated the country as far as Durham; at the beginning of 1070 he marched on Chester and completed the subjugation of the north.

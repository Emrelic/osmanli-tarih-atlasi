The capture of Malacca by the Portuguese

10 August 1511

Malacca, founded in the 1390s, had become the busiest port of the region in the maritime trade among China, India and the West, and, as the centre of the Sultanate of Malacca, the main focus of the spread of Islam to the surrounding islands. The Portuguese Afonso de Albuquerque captured the city on 10 August 1511; the city was plundered, and its mosques, dynastic tombs and other masonry buildings were demolished so that their stones could be used in building a fortress. Malacca under Portuguese rule could not maintain its former commercial pre-eminence; Muslim merchants turned to the port of the Sultanate of Aceh in Sumatra. Upon the attacks launched against Aceh by the Portuguese who had settled in Malaya, the sultan of Aceh, Alâeddin Riâyet Shah, asked Sultan Süleyman the Lawgiver for help in 1565.

The Unilateral Abolition of the Capitulations

9 September 1914

The Ottoman government, at a moment when Europe was occupied with the war, declared that it was abolishing unilaterally, from 1 October 1914, the capitulations that granted foreigners judicial, financial and customs privileges. The ending of this privilege order of four centuries was greeted within the country with great enthusiasm for independence, and celebrations were held in Istanbul. All the states, including its ally Germany, protested the decision; the capitulations would pass into history definitively only in 1923 at Lausanne.

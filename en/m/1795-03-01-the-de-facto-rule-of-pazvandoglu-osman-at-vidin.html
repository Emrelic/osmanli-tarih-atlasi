The De Facto Rule of Pazvandoğlu Osman at Vidin

1 March 1795

Pazvandoğlu Osman, who rose against the Nizam-ı Cedid arrangements, made the governor of Vidin flee, seized the city and fortified it with deep trenches along the Danube. In 1797, at the peak of his power, he gathered a force of nearly eighty thousand and brought Nikopol, Sistova, Ruse (Rusçuk), Sofia and Niš under his control; he presented himself not as a rebel but as a reformer. The Sublime Porte's siege of 1798-1799, lasting months, was fruitless, and until his death in 1807 he ruled in Northern Bulgaria in a semi-independent manner.

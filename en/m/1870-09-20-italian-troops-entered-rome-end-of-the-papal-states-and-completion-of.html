Italian troops entered Rome — end of the Papal States and completion of Italian unity

20 September 1870

When the overthrow of Napoleon III in the Franco-Prussian War led to the withdrawal of the French garrison protecting Rome, Italian troops entered the city and the pope retreated to the Vatican. Thus the Papal States, which had existed for more than a thousand years, came to an end, and the Kingdom of Italy, which also added Rome to the union, came onto the European and world stage as a political power. This is an event with direct consequences for the atlas's last half century: united Italy is the state that would take Tripolitania and the Dodecanese from the Ottomans in 1911.

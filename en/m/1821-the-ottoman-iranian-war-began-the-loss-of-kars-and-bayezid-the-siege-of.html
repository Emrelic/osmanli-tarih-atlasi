The Ottoman-Iranian war began — the loss of Kars and Bayezid, the siege of Erzurum

1821

In the second year of the Greek revolt the eastern frontier of the empire turned into a war zone. The Qajar crown prince Abbas Mirza, using as a pretext the search by Ottoman representatives of the caravan of the shah's wife, who was going on pilgrimage, crossed the frontier with a large military force; he took the provinces of Kars and Bayezid and placed Erzurum under siege. The force facing it was no ordinary frontier unit: Abbas Mirza had trained the army of Azerbaijan according to European methods and had set up a gunpowder workshop and cannon foundries at Tabriz. The Ottoman State, for its part, was at this time occupied in the west with the revolt in the Morea and in the northwest with the resistance of Tepedelenli Ali Pasha at Ioannina, and it had no army trained in the European manner; the Janissary Corps would remain standing until 1826.

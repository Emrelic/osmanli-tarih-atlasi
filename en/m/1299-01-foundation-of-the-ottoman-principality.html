Foundation of the Ottoman Principality

1299

Osman Gazi of the Kayı tribe beginning to act independently around Söğüt and Domaniç is accepted as the conventional starting point for the foundation of the Ottoman state; some historians regard the victory of Bapheus in 1302 as the real turning point. Thanks to its position on the Byzantine frontier, the principality rapidly gathered strength through ghazi raids. In this environment, in which the Seljuk legacy was dissolving, Osman Gazi gathered around himself ahis, ghazis and nomadic Turkmens; his bond with Sheikh Edebali is regarded as the foundation of the principality's spiritual legitimacy. Other places annexed on the same date: İnegöl, Yarhisar.

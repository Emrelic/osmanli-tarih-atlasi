The Return to the Tihama Coast: The Taking of Hodeida, Zabid and Mocha

1849

Forces under Tevfik Pasha, under the supervision of the sharif of Mecca, landed on the Yemeni coast, left ownerless by the withdrawal of the Egyptian army in 1840, and attached the ports of Hodeida, Zabid and Mocha to Ottoman administration. Although it was wished to advance as far as Sana'a, the forces returned to Hodeida because of the resistance of the people; thus the highlands remained with the Zaydi imams, and Ottoman dominion continued for twenty-three years only in the Tihama strip. Inner Yemen would be retaken only in 1872 by the campaign of Ahmed Muhtar Pasha.

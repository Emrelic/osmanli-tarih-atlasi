The Treaty of Istanbul — The Ceding of the Southern Caucasus to Iran

1 September 1736

The second Iranian war, which lasted four years, ended with the treaty signed after the defeat at Arpaçay and the loss of Ganja and Tiflis. The Ottoman State withdrew from all the Southern Caucasian and Western Iranian lands it had taken since 1723, and the frontier returned to the Qasr-i Shirin line of 1639. What remained of thirteen years of eastern campaigns was a heavy financial burden and an emptied treasury; on the other hand, the closing of the eastern front was a vital gain for the new war that had begun with Russia.

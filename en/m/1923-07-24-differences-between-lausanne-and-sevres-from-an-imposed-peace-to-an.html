Differences between Lausanne and Sèvres: from an imposed peace to an equal peace

24 July 1923

Sèvres (10 August 1920) was a document imposed on the Istanbul government and never entered into force; Lausanne (24 July 1923), by contrast, is the peace that Ankara signed as an equal party and that the Grand National Assembly of Turkey ratified on 23 August 1923 (Lausanne art. 1, 143). On borders, Sèvres pulls Turkey's European frontier back to the Çatalca line and gives Eastern Thrace including Edirne, together with Imbros and Tenedos, to Greece (Sèvres art. 27, 84); İzmir and its surroundings remain Turkish in name only while administration is left to Greece, and after five years a local parliament may request annexation (art. 65-83). Lausanne draws the Maritsa-Arda line, Edirne and Karaağaç remain in Turkey, and Imbros and Tenedos become Turkish (Lausanne art. 2, 12, 14; Karaağaç Protocol). On the Straits, Sèvres establishes an international commission extending over all the waters, on which the Turkish member has only one vote; fortifications are razed and the zone is opened to Allied use (Sèvres art. 36-61, 178-180). Lausanne preserves the principle of free passage but leaves a commission with a Turkish chairman, demilitarized zones and a 12,000-man garrison in Istanbul (Lausanne art. 23; Straits Convention). The recognition of Armenia and the arbitration of the border by the US president, as well as the Kurdish autonomy plan (Sèvres art. 62-64, 88-93), do not appear in Lausanne. Regarding the Arab lands, Egypt, Sudan and Cyprus, Sèvres lays down detailed transfer provisions (Sèvres art. 94-97 mandates, art. 98-100 Hejaz, art. 101-122 renunciation of Egypt, Sudan, Cyprus, Morocco, Tunisia and Libya; the TDV's article ranges are the same); Lausanne words the renunciation more briefly, the Dodecanese go to Italy and Cyprus to Britain, and the Mosul border is postponed (Lausanne art. 3, 15-21). Sèvres re-establishes and extends the capitulations, supervises the Turkish budget and borrowing through a Financial Commission, caps the armed force (gendarmerie and reinforcement elements) at 50,000 men, and prohibits military and naval aviation (Sèvres art. 155, 164, 191-195, 231-264, 261-263). Lausanne abolishes the capitulations, apportions the debts and imposes no separate army limit (Lausanne art. 28, 46-63; art. 113 also abolishes foreign post offices). Minority provisions are broader and open to Allied intervention in Sèvres, whereas in Lausanne they are guaranteed by the League of Nations and reciprocal (Sèvres art. 140-151; Lausanne art. 37-45). Axis-by-axis comparison: • Eastern Thrace, Edirne, Çatalca — Sèvres: the Çatalca line is the border; Eastern Thrace and Edirne to Greece (art. 27, 84-85) | Lausanne: border along the Maritsa and Arda; Edirne and Karaağaç remain in Turkey (art. 2; Karaağaç Protocol) • İzmir region — Sèvres: sovereignty Turkish, administration and army Greek, annexation request after five years (art. 65-83) | Lausanne: no special-status article for İzmir; Anatolia lies within Turkey's borders (art. 2-3 general border; no article evidence specific to İzmir) • Straits and Istanbul — Sèvres: International Straits Commission, Turkey 1 vote; razing of fortifications, zone under Allied use; Istanbul in Turkish hands but subject to a minority condition (art. 36-61, 178-180) | Lausanne: principle of free passage; commission with a Turkish chairman, demilitarized zones, 12,000-man garrison in Istanbul (art. 23; Straits Convention) • Armenia and Kurdistan — Sèvres: Armenia recognized, its border determined by the US president; Kurdish autonomy, independence application after one year (art. 62-64, 88-93) | Lausanne: no relevant provision (not found in the summaries of art. 1-143) • Aegean islands, Dodecanese — Sèvres: Imbros, Tenedos and the Aegean islands to Greece; Dodecanese and Kastellorizo to Italy (art. 84, 122) | Lausanne: Imbros and Tenedos in Turkey, Eastern Aegean islands Greek, Dodecanese and Kastellorizo in Italy (art. 12-15) • Cyprus, Egypt, Sudan, Arab lands — Sèvres: mandates over Syria/Mesopotamia and Palestine, Hejaz independent; Egyptian protectorate, Sudan and the annexation of Cyprus recognized (art. 94-98, 101-117) | Lausanne: Syrian border left to the 1921 Franco-Turkish agreement, Iraqi border to later; renunciation of Egypt, Sudan, Cyprus (art. 3, 16-20) • Capitulations — Sèvres: re-established and extended; judicial reform plan in an Allied commission (art. 136, 261-263, 308-309) | Lausanne: abolished entirely; foreign post offices and the High Health Council abolished (art. 28, 113-114) • Financial control and debts — Sèvres: Allied Financial Commission supervises budget and borrowing, the Ottoman Public Debt expands, revenues pledged as security (art. 231-264) | Lausanne: Ottoman Public Debt divided among the states; war damages mutually waived (art. 46-63, 58) • Military restrictions — Sèvres: army of 50,000, air force and tanks prohibited, Allied Control Commissions (art. 152-207) | Lausanne: no general restriction; demilitarization only on the Greek islands and in the Straits and Thrace regions (art. 13; Straits and Thrace conventions) • Minorities — Sèvres: Allied guarantee and right of intervention, annulment of the Abandoned Properties law, restitution (art. 140-151, 86, 142-144) | Lausanne: rights guaranteed by the League of Nations for non-Muslim minorities, reciprocal Muslim rights in Greece; Greek-Turkish population exchange (art. 37-45; Exchange Convention) • Legal status — Sèvres: signed by the Istanbul government, rejected by Ankara, never entered into force (TDV) | Lausanne: equal party; ratification by the Grand National Assembly on 2 August 1923, 213 votes for, 14 against (TDV; art. 1, 143)

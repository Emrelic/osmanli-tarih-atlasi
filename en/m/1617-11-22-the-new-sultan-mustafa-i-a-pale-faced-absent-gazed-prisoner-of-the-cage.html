The new sultan Mustafa I: a pale-faced, absent-gazed prisoner of the 'cage' on the throne

22 November 1617

Mustafa I, who came to the throne on the death of Ahmed I, had lived for years shut up in the palace under close watch as the first prince his brother had left alive; the TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi describes him as 'pale-faced, thin-bearded, with large black eyes and a vacant gaze' and records that this long confinement worsened his psychological condition. In this first reign, lasting ninety-six days, he was unable to conduct affairs of state; on the other hand it is related that he often went hunting, toured the arsenal, and even threw gold into the water to watch the fish. The Janissary corps and the ulema, regarding him as unfit for sovereignty, deposed him on 26 February 1618 and placed his fourteen-year-old nephew Osman II on the throne.

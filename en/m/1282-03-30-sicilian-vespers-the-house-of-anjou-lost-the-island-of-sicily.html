Sicilian Vespers — the House of Anjou lost the island of Sicily

30 March 1282

The uprising that broke out in Palermo at the hour of the evening prayer on Easter Monday against the rule of Charles I of Anjou tore the island of Sicily from the House of Anjou. The Kingdom of Sicily was divided in two: the island turned to Aragon, while the House of Anjou held on in the mainland kingdom centred on Naples. The dynasty's overseas possessions in the Adriatic and the Mediterranean also remained attached to this kingdom: Corfu, which they had held since 1267, and Durazzo, which they had held since the 1270s, continued as part of the Kingdom of Naples; the fortresses of Valona, Kanina and Himara on the Albanian coast also remained in the Naples-centred kingdom (the commander of Valona, John of Taxis, resisted the Byzantines throughout 1282; Durazzo, Valona and Kanina most probably passed to Byzantium in early 1285). Malta, however, fell into the hands of the Aragonese only in 1284.

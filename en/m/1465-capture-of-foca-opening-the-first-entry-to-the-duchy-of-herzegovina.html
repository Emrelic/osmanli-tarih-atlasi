Capture of Foča: opening the first entry to the Duchy of Herzegovina

1465

After the Kingdom of Bosnia was abolished in 1463, it was the turn of the lands of Stjepan Vukčić Kosača, who had separated from the kingdom and founded his own duchy. Foča, situated in the Drina valley at the junction of the trade route linking Istanbul to Dubrovnik, came under Ottoman administration in 1465. The importance of the city was immediately understood: in 1470 it was made the center of the newly organized sancak of Herzegovina — that is, the administrative core of Herzegovina was established at Foča before the duchy itself had even fallen. The annexation of the whole duchy would continue until 1483. Foča thereafter developed as a center of trade and administration thanks to its favorable climate and its position at a road junction. On the map this date is the day the second wave southward after the conquest of Bosnia began.

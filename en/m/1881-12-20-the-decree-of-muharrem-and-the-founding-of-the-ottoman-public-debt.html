The Decree of Muharrem and the Founding of the Ottoman Public Debt Administration

20 December 1881

The negotiations conducted with the representatives of the creditor states after the bankruptcy of 1875 ended in the decree dated 28 Muharrem 1299 (20 December 1881): the debt principal was reduced by more than half, and in return the most productive revenue items, such as the salt, tobacco, silk, stamp and fish duties, were transferred to the Düyûn-ı Umûmiyye İdaresi (Public Debt Administration), directly managed by the creditors. This administration, which employed thousands of officials, turned into a separate financial organisation within the state. It is accepted as the date on which Ottoman financial independence effectively ended.

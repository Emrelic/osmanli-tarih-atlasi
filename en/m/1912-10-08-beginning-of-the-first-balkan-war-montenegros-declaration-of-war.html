Beginning of the First Balkan War — Montenegro's declaration of war

8 October 1912

The Balkan states, after the Committee of Union and Progress resolved the question of the churches with the law of 3 July 1911, had removed the greatest obstacle among them and, at Russia's instigation, had signed alliance treaties with one another throughout 1912 (Serbia-Bulgaria on 13 March, Bulgaria-Greece on 29 May, Montenegro-Bulgaria in August, Montenegro-Serbia on 6 October). The Sublime Porte did not notice this; indeed, relying on Russia's guarantee, it discharged the 120 trained battalions in Rumelia. The four states presented a joint note on 3 October demanding autonomy for Macedonia, Albania and Crete; receiving no reply, the operations began on 8 October 1912 with Montenegro's declaration of war. On 13 October the Serbian and Bulgarian envoys were handed their passports, and the next day these two states, followed by Greece, declared war. During the war the political differences within the army played a great part in the defeat.

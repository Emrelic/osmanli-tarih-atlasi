The shifting of the Ibrim and Nubian frontier to the south

1555

The southern frontier of the beylerbeylik of Egypt was moved beyond the Second Cataract when the fortress of Ibrim, rising on a rocky promontory on the Nile, was fortified by Özdemir Bey and Lower Nubia was fully incorporated into Ottoman Egypt. (The Ottoman administrative organisation in Ibrim had been established after the conquest of Egypt; on the map Ibrim is shown as Ottoman from 1517. This year is approximate; the source gives no year.) The fortress was as much a barrier against raids coming from Nubia as it was a control point on the gold and slave route to Sennar. The Bosnian garrison stationed there became so permanent that their descendants were still in the fortress three hundred years later. The sources give two different accounts of when Ibrim came under Ottoman administration: the TDV article on Sudan links the incorporation of Lower Nubia into Ottoman Egypt to the operations of Özdemir Pasha in the middle of the 16th century, whereas the article on Nubia writes that the region as far as the Wadi Halfa valley and the administrative organisation in Ibrim were established immediately after the conquest of 1517. The sancak of Ibrim was attached to the province of Habeş on 26 July 1573.

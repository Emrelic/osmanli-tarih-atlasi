The Conquest of Yanova (Ineu) and the Transylvanian Campaign

27 August 1658

Köprülü Mehmed Pasha, who set out on campaign to punish George II Rákóczi, Prince of Transylvania, who had violated Ottoman protection by setting out on an unauthorised Polish campaign, marched by way of Belgrade and took the strategic fortress of Yanova. A new prince was appointed to Transylvania, and the vassalage bond was sharply recalled. This campaign was the first link in the Ottomans' attempt to re-establish their authority in Central Europe. Transylvania was already a principality subject to the Ottomans; the capture of Yanova was not a conquest from a foreign state, but the removal of this fortress of vassal Transylvania from the principality's administration and its attachment to an Ottoman sancak directly; the rest of the principality remained in vassal status.

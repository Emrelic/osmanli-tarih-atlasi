Corsica sold by Genoa to France — Treaty of Versailles

15 May 1768

The Republic of Genoa ceded to France Corsica, which it had held for centuries but could no longer effectively control because of revolts. The TDV entry on Corsica gives the event in a single sentence: Genoa, unable to hold on to this island, sold it to France in 1768. The transfer marks Genoa's withdrawal from its last major territorial holding in the Mediterranean; the island will pass to the British for a time in 1796 and then be returned to France. That the island was French territory made Napoleon Bonaparte, born in Ajaccio a year later, a French subject. In this year (1768) Corsica, including Bastia, was sold by Genoa to France (TDV Korsika; the day could not be verified).

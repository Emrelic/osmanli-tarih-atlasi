Treaty of Tilsit — Prussia lost half its territory, the Duchy of Warsaw was founded

9 July 1807

After the Jena-Auerstedt rout, Prussia had to accept a heavy peace with Napoleon at Tilsit (9 July 1807). It gave up all its lands west of the Elbe and the Polish lands it had received in the 1793 and 1795 partitions; from the latter the Duchy of Warsaw was founded and the area around Białystok was given to Russia. Prussia lost about half its population and territory, and its army was limited to 42,000. In the atlas, Poznań and Toruń pass to the Duchy of Warsaw from this day, and Białystok to Russia. By this treaty Łódź and Częstochowa (Polish provinces that passed to Prussia after 1772) passed to the Duchy of Warsaw; Gdańsk (Danzig) became an independent free city.

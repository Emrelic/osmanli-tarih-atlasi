Evacuation of Silistra — the middle fortress of the Danube abandoned with its walls demolished

1 May 1811

Silistra, one of the strongest Ottoman fortresses on the Danube, had been bombarded by Russian artillery in 1810, and the fortress and suburb had been heavily damaged by the explosion of an arsenal. After taking the city the Russians chose not to hold it but to destroy its fortifications: General Kutuzov ordered the demolition of the walls and in May 1811 reported to the tsar that the walls of Silistra had been blown up. The TDV's record: "In 1810 the city was bombarded by the Russians… After the Russians took the city and before their withdrawal, General Kutuzov ordered the demolition of Silistra. In May 1811 he informed the tsar that the walls of Silistra had been blown up." The same fortress would be retaken by the Russians in the war of 1827-1828, and Helmuth von Moltke would describe that siege in detail.

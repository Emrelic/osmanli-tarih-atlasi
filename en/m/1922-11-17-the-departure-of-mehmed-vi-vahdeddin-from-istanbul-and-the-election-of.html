The Departure of Mehmed VI Vahdeddin from Istanbul and the Election of Abdülmecid Efendi as Caliph

17 November 1922

Mehmed VI Vahdeddin, the last sultan, whose position had become disputed after the abolition of the sultanate, left Istanbul on the morning of Friday, 17 November 1922, aboard the British battleship Malaya and went to Malta. Two days later, on 19 November, the TBMM elected Abdülmecid Efendi as a caliph without political authority. The dynastic rule of the Ottomans, lasting six hundred and twenty-three years, thus effectively ended; the caliphate would continue symbolically for fifteen more months. Vahdeddin, who went to Malta and then to Italy, settled at the Villa Nobel in San Remo; his applications to be accepted into a Muslim country brought no result, and he died there on 16 May 1926, reiterating that he had never renounced his rights of sultanate and caliphate.

The Byzantine Empire became a tributary of the Ottomans

1372

After the victory at Chirmen (1371), the Byzantine emperor John V agreed in the winter of 1372 to become a tributary of Murad I: he would pay an annual tribute and furnish troops for the sultan's campaigns. From this date until 1403 Byzantium remained a vassal state dependent on the Ottomans; Bayezid the Thunderbolt's blockade of 1391 increased the tribute. After the Ankara defeat, the Treaty of Gallipoli (1403) with Süleyman Çelebi freed Byzantium from tribute; with the treaty made with Murad II in 1424 the tribute began again.

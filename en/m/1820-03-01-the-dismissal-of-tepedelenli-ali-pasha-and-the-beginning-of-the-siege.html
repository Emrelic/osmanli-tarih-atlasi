The dismissal of Tepedelenli Ali Pasha and the beginning of the siege of Ioannina

1 March 1820

Mahmud II's policy of eliminating local powers reached Tepedelenli Ali Pasha, who had governed Ioannina for thirty-three years and held Thessaly and Epirus in effect in his own hands. The pasha was dismissed from the headship of the derbends, and his son Veli from the pashalik of Trikala; since it was expected that he would not comply, forces were dispatched against him. Hurşid Ahmed Pasha, who was charged with this task, was at the same time governor of the Morea and had also been given the post of serasker of Rumelia; all civil and military administrators in the region were charged with capturing Tepedelenli. That is, when the Greek revolt broke out, the commander who should have been governing the Morea and the forces under him were besieging the fortress of Ioannina. On 23 May 1820 Ali Pasha held a meeting with the Greeks at Ioannina, attempted to establish a Greek province under his own administration and gave them aid in money and arms. The opposing side was no negligible power: Ali Pasha had an army of 30,000-40,000, of whom 7,000 were regulars, and a small fleet; the population under his administration was estimated at 1.5-2 million and his annual revenue at 4-5 million kuruş.

Russia's declaration of war: the beginning of the '93 War' (Russo-Turkish War of 1877-1878)

24 April 1877

The suppression of the Bulgarian Uprising of 1876 had aroused great indignation in European public opinion, and Russia had exploited it to the full to isolate the Sublime Porte. When the Istanbul (Tersane) Conference's proposal to divide Bulgaria into two autonomous provinces, and then the London Protocol of 31 March 1877, were rejected by the Ottoman State, Russia declared war on 24 April 1877 and its army entered the territory of the Principality of Romania, which it had drawn to its side (the map shows Wallachia-Moldavia from this moment until the Armistice of Edirne with the hatched overlay of the Russian advance). Because it coincided with the year 1293 in the Rumi calendar, the war is known as the '93 War' (Doksanüç Harbi). The operations were conducted on two fronts, the Danube and Eastern Anatolia: on the Danube, the Ottoman army of 180,000 men had counted the left bank of the river as the first, and the Balkan mountains as the second line of defense; in the east, Ahmed Muhtar Pasha's 55,000 soldiers were positioned between Ardahan and Doğubayazıt. On the map this war is the event that began the collapse of Ottoman Rumelia — the present borders of Bulgaria, Serbia, Montenegro and Romania are all products of these fifteen months.

The construction of the fortress of Anapa — the Caucasus defence line

1781

When Crimea was lost with Küçük Kaynarca, the construction of a new fortress at the mouth of the Kuban River was undertaken in order to preserve Ottoman influence in the Caucasus. Anapa, founded on the remains of a Genoese colony, with its bazaar, its baths and its more than two hundred and fifty shops, soon became a city that provided the link with the Circassian tribes. The fortress remained for forty-eight years the Ottomans' most advanced outpost against Russia's descent into the Caucasus.

The Italian occupation of the Dodecanese

12 May 1912

Within a week of the taking of Rhodes, the Italian fleet also occupied the surrounding islands; this archipelago would later be given the name 'Dodecanese'. Most of the islands were a few miles from the Anatolian coast and controlled the southeastern passage of the Aegean. The occupation was regarded as temporary in the Treaty of Ouchy in return for the abandonment of Tripolitania, but Italy never evacuated the islands; they would be handed over to Greece in 1947.

Founding of Santa Cruz do Cabo de Gué: the Portuguese fortress at Agadir

1505

The fortress of Santa Cruz do Cabo de Gué, established through private enterprise at a point on the Sous coast that Portugal had not reached until then, was built in 1505 and transferred to the Portuguese crown in 1513. The fortress, controlling the sugar and gold trade of the Sous valley, closed the southwestern outlet of Marrakesh; for this reason it became the first great target of the Saadian movement. The siege of 1533 was repelled, the one of 1541 succeeded and began Portugal's collapse in Morocco.

The Sinop Raid

30 November 1853

The Russian Black Sea fleet, under the command of Admiral Nakhimov, raided the Ottoman fleet anchored in the port of Sinop without a declaration of war; twelve ships sank, more than two thousand soldiers fell as martyrs, and the fleet commander Osman Pasha was taken prisoner. According to the TDV Encyclopaedia of Islam article on Sinop, in the raid seven mosques, two schools, 247 houses and 170 shops in the town also burned, and sixteen civilians, five of them Muslim and eleven Christian, lost their lives. The event, which echoed in European public opinion as the 'Sinop disaster', was the direct trigger of Britain and France's entering the war on the Ottoman side within a few months.

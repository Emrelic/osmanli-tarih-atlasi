Treaty of Nystad — the Great Northern War ended, the Baltic coasts passed to Russia

10 September 1721

The Treaty of Nystad, which ended the 21-year Great Northern War, was signed in Finland on 10 September 1721 (Julian 30 August); Sweden left Estonia, Livonia (including Riga), Ingria and part of southeastern Finland (Karelia) to Russia, in return recovering the rest of Finland, and compensation was paid. The treaty is regarded as the end of Sweden's great-power status and the official registration of Russia's rise as a European empire — Peter I took the title of 'Emperor' after this victory.

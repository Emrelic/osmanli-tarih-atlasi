Dissolution of the Norway-Sweden Union — the Storting's unilateral declaration of independence

7 June 1905

After King Oscar II vetoed the Norwegian government's demand for a separate consulate service and declared that he could not form a new government, the Norwegian Storting (parliament) resolved unanimously on 7 June 1905 that the King had ceased to function as King of Norway and that the union, which had lasted since the 1814 Treaty of Kiel, was dissolved. In the plebiscite of 13 August 1905, almost all voters (184 against 371,000) approved the dissolution; Sweden formally recognized independence on 26 October 1905, following the Karlstad negotiations.

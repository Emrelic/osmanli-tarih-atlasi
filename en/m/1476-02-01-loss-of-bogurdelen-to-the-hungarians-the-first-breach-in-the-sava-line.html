Loss of Böğürdelen to the Hungarians: the first breach in the Sava line

1 February 1476

A fortress of wood and earth had been built in 1471, in the time of Mehmed the Conqueror, at a position that had come into Ottoman hands and had earlier been called Zaslon. Because it stood at a point threatening Belgrade and raids could easily be made from it on the southern regions of Hungary and Austria, the fortress also took its name from this function — Böğürdelen means 'bastion striking from the flank.' The Hungarian King Matthias Corvinus besieged the fortress toward the end of 1475 and took it in February 1476. The Hungarian epic Szabács viadala, which recounts the siege, is among the longest surviving texts of fifteenth-century Hungarian historical poems. The Hungarians established a banate in the region and rebuilt the fortress in stone, making it a link in the chain of frontier fortresses. The Ottomans tried to retake it in 1492 and did not succeed; the fortress remained in Hungarian hands for forty-five years.

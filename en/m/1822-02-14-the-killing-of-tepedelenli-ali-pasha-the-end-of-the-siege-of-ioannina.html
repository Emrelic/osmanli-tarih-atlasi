The killing of Tepedelenli Ali Pasha — the end of the siege of Ioannina

14 February 1822

The resistance at Ioannina lasted nearly two years, and within this very period the Greek revolt took on a general extent. When Hurşid Pasha sent spies into the fortress and distributed ranks and gifts to those who fled, the number of soldiers around Ali Pasha fell to forty or fifty; the pasha withdrew to the Monastery of Pandeleimon on the lake, with a guarantee that his life would not be touched if he surrendered. At this time Hurşid Pasha's family had fallen into the hands of the Greek rebels; in order to return to the Morea as soon as possible, he disregarded the safe-conduct he had given and had a fabricated execution decree prepared. When his room was entered, Ali Pasha answered with arms and was killed in the ensuing fighting; his head was brought to Istanbul on 24 February. The siege had also revealed the rift within the Ottoman command: when the Morea revolt became more dangerous than the Tepedelenli question, Seyyid Ali Pasha was appointed independent serasker of the Morea, and Hurşid Pasha, who disliked this, rejected his requests for help and even tried to make him appear a failure in the eyes of the state. At the end of the year in which Ali Pasha was removed, Nafplion (Anabolu), the strongest fortress of the Morea, fell.

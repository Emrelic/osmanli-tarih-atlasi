General Maritime Treaty — the Qawasim and the other Gulf sheikhdoms bound by treaty to Britain

8 January 1820

After bombarding Ras al-Khaimah in 1819 and scattering the Qasimi 'pirate' fleet, Britain imposed the General Maritime Treaty, which began on 8 January 1820 and was completed by March, being signed with the sheikhdoms of the region one by one; the treaty prohibited piracy and slavery and required ships to carry a red-and-white flag similar to the British flag. With this treaty the 'Pirate Coast' gradually became the 'Trucial Coast'; British hegemony, deepened by the 1853 perpetual maritime truce and the 1892 exclusive agreements, was the beginning of a relationship that would last until 1971.

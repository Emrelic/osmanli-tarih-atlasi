British civil mandate administration established in Palestine — end of military rule in the lands taken from the Ottomans

1 July 1920

With General Allenby's capture of Gaza and then Jerusalem in 1917, four hundred years of Ottoman rule in Palestine had ended, and Jerusalem remained under British military administration in 1917-1920. The 1920 San Remo Conference gave Palestine to the British mandate; from July 1920 Britain established a civil mandate administration in the region and began to govern the country through a high commissioner. At the head of the civil administration was the high commissioner Herbert Samuel. The Arab uprisings and Jewish-Arab clashes that began in the same year would mark the whole of the mandate period. With this event Tiberias (Palestine) passed to British civil mandate administration.

Entry into the First World War

1 November 1914

The admission through the Straits of the battleships Goeben and Breslau, which had escaped from Britain, by presenting them as purchased, drew the state step by step to Germany's side; the war effectively began with the fleet under Admiral Souchon bombarding Russian ports (29 October), and the mutual declarations came in early November. Despite the proclamation of the Great Jihad (Cihâd-ı Ekber), the expected effect did not materialize; for four years the empire fought a struggle for survival on five separate fronts, from the Caucasus to Palestine.

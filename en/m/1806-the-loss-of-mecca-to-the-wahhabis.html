The loss of Mecca to the Wahhabis

1806

The Wahhabi movement, born in Najd, turned toward the Hejaz in the time of Saud b. Abd al-Aziz; after Ta'if and Medina, Mecca was also taken for a second time — Sharif Ghalib, the Emir of Mecca, surrendered the city to the Wahhabis on condition of remaining in the emirate. The tombs were destroyed, and the road of the pilgrim caravans coming from Damascus and Cairo was cut; the surre that the sultan sent every year to the Two Holy Cities was also interrupted in these years — the single exception to this allocation, which continued uninterrupted until 1915, is the period of Wahhabi domination. Because the title of protector of the Two Holy Cities was one of the main supports of the sultan's legitimacy, this loss was, beyond a territorial loss, a heavy political blow; its recovery would take seven years and would be possible with the army of Mehmed Ali Pasha of Kavala.

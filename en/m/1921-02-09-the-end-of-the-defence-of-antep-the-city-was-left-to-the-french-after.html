The end of the defence of Antep — the city was left to the French after resisting for ten months

9 February 1921

Antep, which the British entered on 17 December 1918, was left to the French on 5 November 1919; the first resistance on the Antep–Kilis line was led by Şahin Bey. According to the TDV article on Gaziantep, the people of the city resisted the French forces from 1 April 1920 to 7 February 1921; when the resistance was broken, the defending forces withdrew and the French took control of the city on 9 February. The Grand National Assembly had granted the city, which held out against the occupation for ten months, the title of gazi three days earlier, on 6 February 1921. Antep would be evacuated on 25 December 1921 following the Ankara Agreement.

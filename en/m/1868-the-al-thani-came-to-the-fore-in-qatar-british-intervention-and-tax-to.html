The Al Thani came to the fore in Qatar — British intervention and tax to Bahrain

1868

The Al Thani, who acted as tax collectors in the region on behalf of the Sheikhdom of Bahrain and the Emirate of Saud, became decisive in Qatari politics from the 1860s. When the rivalries within the Al Saud in Najd and the Al Khalifa in Bahrain made Qatar important to the British, Britain sent a ship to Qatar in the autumn of 1868 and obliged Muhammad b. Thani to pay tax to the Bahraini emirs. This intervention began the process leading to the 1871 Lahsa campaign of Midhat Pasha, governor of Baghdad. With this event the Qatar peninsula came to the fore as a separate unit under Al Thani (Muhammad b. Thani) rule.

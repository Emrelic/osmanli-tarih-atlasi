Siege of Konya and treaty with the Karamanids — Hamid-ili, Said-ili, Beyşehir, Seydişehir and Akşehir recovered

1 March 1415

Çelebi Mehmed mounted a great campaign against the Karamanids, who had attacked Ottoman territory and besieged and burned Bursa during the Interregnum, and besieged Konya in Muharram 818. Karamanid Mehmed Bey II was compelled to ask for peace; as a result of the treaty, Hamid-ili (the Isparta region) and Said-ili rejoined Ottoman lands, Beyşehir, Seydişehir and Akşehir were also given back, and Sivrihisar, which Timur had given the Karamanids in 1402, also returned to the Ottomans. This campaign, in which the Anatolian unity shattered by the Battle of Ankara began to be patched together, is among the concrete grounds for regarding Çelebi Mehmed as the 'second founder'.

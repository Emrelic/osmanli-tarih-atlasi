Assassination of Nadir Shah: dissolution of Afsharid rule

20 June 1747

Nader Shah, who had put an end to Safavid rule and proclaimed his own sultanate in 1736, marched as far as Delhi in his Indian campaign, and fought three wars with the Ottomans before making peace with the 1746 Treaty of Kerden, was killed in his tent by his own guards. Iran then could not be governed by a single hand: for forty-nine years an Afsharid remnant in Khorasan, the Zands in the south, and the rising Qajars in the north shared the country. Thanks to this fragmentation the Ottoman eastern frontier remained quiet for a long time. Nader Shah was killed on 20 June 1747; according to TDV, the Erivan (Revan) Khanate was founded in the same year (1747) (the day could not be verified).

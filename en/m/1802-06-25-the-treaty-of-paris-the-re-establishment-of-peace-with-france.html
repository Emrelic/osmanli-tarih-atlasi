The Treaty of Paris — The Re-establishment of Peace with France

25 June 1802

By the treaty signed after the evacuation of Egypt, the state of war between the Ottoman State and France officially ended and the old capitulations were renewed. French merchant ships were permitted to enter the Black Sea; in return France recognised the territorial integrity of the Ottomans. The establishment of peace gave room for manoeuvre again to the foreign policy of Selim III, which had been squeezed between Russia and England.

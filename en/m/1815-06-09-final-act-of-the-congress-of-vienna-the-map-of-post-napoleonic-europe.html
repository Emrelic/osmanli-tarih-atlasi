Final Act of the Congress of Vienna — the map of post-Napoleonic Europe redrawn

9 June 1815

The Final Act of the Congress of Vienna, signed among the great powers that ended the Napoleonic Wars (Austria, Russia, Prussia, Britain, France), was adopted on 9 June 1815 (9 days before Waterloo) and collectively redrew the map of the continent: the former Spanish/Austrian Netherlands and the United Provinces were united under the 'United Kingdom of the Netherlands', the Republic of Genoa joined the Kingdom of Sardinia-Piedmont, most of the Duchy of Warsaw was attached to Russia under the name 'Congress Poland' and Swedish Pomerania (Stralsund) passed to Prussia. This single day is regarded as the official beginning of the post-war European balance (Concert of Europe). By this act Łódź, Częstochowa, Kielce, Radom, Lublin, Chełm and Zamość (Duchy of Warsaw) passed to the Kingdom of Poland attached to Russia, Poznań (Grand Duchy of Posen) to Prussia, and Maastricht and Roermond to the Kingdom of the Netherlands.

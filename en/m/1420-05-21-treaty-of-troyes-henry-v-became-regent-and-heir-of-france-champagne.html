Treaty of Troyes: Henry V became regent and heir of France, Champagne came under English-Burgundian rule: Troyes

21 May 1420

The treaty signed at Troyes on 21 May 1420 between Charles VI and Henry V, King of England, made Henry V regent of France and heir to the throne; the Dauphin Charles (Charles VII) was excluded from the inheritance. The treaty transfers no territory; Champagne (Troyes, Reims) remained from this date until 1429 under English-Burgundian ('Lancastrian France') administration, and the map shows this administration as England.

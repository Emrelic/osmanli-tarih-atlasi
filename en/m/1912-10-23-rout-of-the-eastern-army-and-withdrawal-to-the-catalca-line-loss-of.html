Rout of the Eastern Army and withdrawal to the Çatalca line: loss of Kumanovo and Salonica

23 October 1912

The Ottoman Eastern Army, defeated on 23 October 1912 by a Bulgarian army three times its size, withdrew as far as the Çatalca line, thirty kilometers west of Istanbul; the capital came under direct threat in the first month of the war. In the same days the Western Army was defeated by the Serbs at Kumanovo on 23-24 October, and Tahsin Pasha surrendered to the Greeks at Salonica with his army of 35,000 men. Upon these routs the cabinet of Gazi Ahmed Muhtar Pasha resigned on 29 October; Abdulhamid II, who was living in exile in Salonica, was transferred to Istanbul on 1 November. The Çatalca line was held until the end of the war — the five-hundred-year-old Ottoman geography of Rumelia shrank within a few weeks to this narrow strip.

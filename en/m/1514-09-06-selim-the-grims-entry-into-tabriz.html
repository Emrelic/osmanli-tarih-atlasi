Selim the Grim's Entry into Tabriz

6 September 1514

Following the victory at Chaldiran, the Ottoman army entered the Safavid capital Tabriz, and the khutba was read in the city in the name of the sultan. Selim stayed there for a week and had some of the artists and scholars (ulema) of Tabriz transferred to Istanbul; this transfer was to have a profound influence on Ottoman court art. Although the city was abandoned as winter approached and because of the discontent of the Janissaries, Safavid superiority in the East had been broken. The places that passed to the Ottomans on the map on this day are not contiguous with one another, and this is correct: Tabriz is a nine-day army occupation; the plain of Erzincan surrendered without a fight during the campaign, while the fortress of Kemah in its midst held out as Safavid until 19 May 1515; Siirt was recovered from the Safavids by Halil Bey, the ruler (melik) of Hasankeyf, who had been freed from Shah Ismail's imprisonment in Tabriz and submitted to the Ottomans — Diyarbakır around it remained Safavid until 1515, and Hasankeyf until 1517.

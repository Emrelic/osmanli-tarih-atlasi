The Erzurum and Van basin passed to the Safavids: the northern flank of the Aqquyunlu collapsed

1502

After defeating Alvand Bey at Sharur and entering Tabriz in the summer of 1501, Shah Ismail advanced northwest the following year and brought under his rule the line stretching from Erzurum to the Lake Van basin. After the death of Uzun Hasan, the Akkoyunlu State had already been split in two by throne struggles: Alvand Bey held Azerbaijan and Diyarbakır, while his cousin Murad held Persian Iraq and Fars. This division eased the Safavid advance; the region changed hands within a year. Erzurum remained in Safavid hands for sixteen years and came under Ottoman rule only after Çaldıran, in 1518-19. In this year (1502), Aşkale and Pasinler (Hasankale), together with the Erzurum region, passed from the Akkoyunlu to the Safavids (the day could not be verified).

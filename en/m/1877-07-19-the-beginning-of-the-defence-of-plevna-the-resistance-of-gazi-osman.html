The Beginning of the Defence of Plevna: The Resistance of Gazi Osman Pasha

19 July 1877

Osman Pasha, who marched from Vidin to Plevna in order to block the Russian army that had crossed the Danube in the '93 War from the road to Istanbul, fortified the town with redoubts and trenches and began a legendary defence of five months. Three successive great Russian-Romanian assaults were repulsed with heavy losses, and the army, directed in person by Tsar Alexander II, was obliged to settle for a siege. Plevna went down in history as the Ottoman army's most brilliant defensive success of the nineteenth century.

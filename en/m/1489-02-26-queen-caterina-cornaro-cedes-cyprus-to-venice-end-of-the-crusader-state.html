Queen Caterina Cornaro cedes Cyprus to Venice — end of the Crusader state

26 February 1489

Caterina Cornaro of Venice, who had governed Cyprus as queen regent after the death of her husband King James II in 1473, abdicated on 26 February 1489 under pressure from the Venetian government, since she had no heir, and transferred her sovereign rights on the island to the doge and the Venetian Republic; in return she retained the title of queen and received the county of Asolo on the Terraferma. With this transfer, the rule of the Lusignan dynasty, which had lasted since 1192, and the last Crusader state came to an end; the island remained under Venetian administration until the Ottoman conquest of 1570-71.

The Treaty of Istanbul: Peace with Bulgaria and the Registration of Edirne

29 September 1913

The treaty signed between the Ottoman State and Bulgaria after the Second Balkan War, accepting the Maritsa river as the frontier, legally registered the remaining of Edirne, Kırklareli, Dimetoka and Eastern Thrace with the Ottomans. Dedeağaç was left in Bulgaria (Kavala, however, had passed to Greece in July 1913 after the Bulgarian occupation of the autumn of 1912 and had been registered in Greece by the Treaty of Bucharest of 10 August 1913), and a mutual exchange of populations between the two countries and the rights of the Muslims in Bulgaria were regulated. This text is the guarantee of the only piece of land that could be recovered from the Balkan catastrophe. Other places that joined on the same date: Gümülcine.

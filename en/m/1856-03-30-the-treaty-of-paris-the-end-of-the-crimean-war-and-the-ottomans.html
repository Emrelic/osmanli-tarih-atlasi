The Treaty of Paris: The End of the Crimean War and the Ottomans' Admission to the European System of States

30 March 1856

With the Treaty of Paris, which ended the Crimean War, the Ottoman State was declared an equal member of European public law and its territorial integrity was placed under the joint guarantee of the great powers. The Black Sea was neutralised, Russia's claim of protection over Orthodox subjects, which it had held since Küçük Kaynarca, was invalidated, and the south of Bessarabia (the kazas of Kilia, Cahul, Ismail and Bolgrad) was given to Moldavia. However, by making the Reform Edict an international commitment, the same treaty also opened the door to interference in internal affairs.

The retaking of Tenedos and Imbros — the Treaty of Athens

1 November 1913

Of the North Aegean islands occupied by Greece in the Balkan War, Tenedos and Imbros, which hold the mouth of the Straits, were regarded as indispensable for the defence of Çanakkale during the negotiations for the Treaty of Athens and were returned to the Ottomans. The other islands remained with Greece. These two islands became the last territories the Empire held in the Aegean, and at Lausanne they were also left to Turkey.

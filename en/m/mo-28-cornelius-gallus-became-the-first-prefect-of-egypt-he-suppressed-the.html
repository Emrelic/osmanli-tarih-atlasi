Cornelius Gallus became the first prefect of Egypt; he suppressed the revolts of Heroonpolis and Thebes, and the frontier with Kush was secured

28 BC

Octavian made Egypt a special tributary province and left its administration not to a senator but to a prefect of the equestrian class; the first prefect was Cornelius Gallus. Strabo writes that Gallus took a rebellious town at Heroonpolis with a small number of soldiers and quickly suppressed the uprising at Thebes caused by taxation. The Gallus stele at Philae reports that envoys of the Kushite ruler were received there and that the region of Triakontaschoinos was placed under protection.

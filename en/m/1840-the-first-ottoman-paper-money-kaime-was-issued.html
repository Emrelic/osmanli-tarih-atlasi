The first Ottoman paper money (kâime) was issued

1840

In the early years of the Tanzimat, while the search for revenue for the treasury was under way, the practice of the kâime, an improved form of the esham system, was adopted on the proposal of Ârif Hikmet Bey, who would later become şeyhülislam. The first kâimes, totalling 160,000 liras (32,000 purses), were issued handwritten, without waiting to be printed, because of the urgent need for money, probably in June 1840 under the Minister of Finance Sâib Pasha. These papers, which had the force of cash, were to circulate for eight years and bore interest of 12.5 per cent a year; they had no backing, and only their interest was secured from the revenue of the Istanbul customs. The public did not receive this unfamiliar money favourably, and the handwritten kâimes soon became a target of counterfeiters.

Fall of the Kingdom of Bosnia — the Conqueror's Bosnian campaign

1 June 1463

When the last Bosnian king, Stjepan Tomašević (1461-1463), relying on Western support, refused to pay tribute, he was deemed to have ceased to be a vassal ruler, and the army under the Conqueror, Sultan Mehmed, liquidated the Kingdom of Bosnia in a single campaign. The king was captured and executed, the kingdom disappeared and the sancak of Bosnia was established in the region. But the fall of the kingdom is not the same thing as the conquest of Bosnia. Ottoman administration had already existed in the plain for fifteen years — Hodidjed and the plain of Saray had been taken in 1448, and the frontier lordship of Îsâ Bey had been established in the middle of the kingdom. The conquest did not end in 1463 either: Foča was taken in 1465, Livno in 1469, the Duchy of Herzegovina in 1483, the banate of Srebrenik in 1512, and Jajce and Banja Luka in the north only in 1528 after Mohács — the Hungarians had established a banate in northern Bosnia that held out for sixty-five years after 1463. The place that changed hands directly on this date is Travnik; Jajce passed on this date not to the Ottomans but to Hungary.

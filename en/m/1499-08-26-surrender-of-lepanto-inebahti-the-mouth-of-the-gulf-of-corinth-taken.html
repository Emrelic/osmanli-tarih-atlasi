Surrender of Lepanto (Inebahtı): the mouth of the Gulf of Corinth taken from Venice

26 August 1499

Lepanto had been in Venetian hands since 1407 and held the mouth of the Gulf of Corinth; the entire coast inside the gulf lay within the range of this fortress. In the campaign of 1499 the fortress was besieged by land, and the Ottoman fleet, which fought the Venetian fleet off Lepanto, assisted the besieging forces from the sea. The Venetians in the fortress surrendered the town to the Ottomans on 26 August. The city then remained Ottoman for one hundred and eighty-eight years, passed back to Venice in 1687 with the loss of the Morea, and was retaken in 1715. The naval battle of the same summer and this surrender are two separate events: the fleet engagement took place at the western tip of the Morea, in the waters of Sapienza-Zonchio; Lepanto, on the other hand, fell to a land siege at the mouth of the Gulf of Corinth.

Loss of the Fortress of Azak to the Don Cossacks

18 June 1637

The Don Cossacks took the Fortress of Azak, which they had besieged on 21 April 1637, two months later; the fortress, which was the northeastern gate of the Black Sea and the lock of the Don trade, left Ottoman hands for the first time. According to the Azak entry of the TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, the Cossacks took the fortress in 1637, withstood in 1641 a great three-month siege under the command of Deli Hüseyin Pasha, and evacuated the fortress only in 1642 with the arrival of the Crimean Khan Mehmed Giray. The state was at that time preoccupied with the Baghdad front; the failure to hold the fortress for five years is the first crack in the Black Sea's character as an 'Ottoman lake.'

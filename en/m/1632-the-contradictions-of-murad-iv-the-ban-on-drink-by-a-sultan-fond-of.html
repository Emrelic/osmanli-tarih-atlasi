The contradictions of Murad IV: the ban on drink by a sultan fond of drink, a wrestler's wrist, a composer's ear

1632

In the early years of Murad IV, who came to the throne at the age of eleven, the influence of his mother Kösem Sultan was decisive; the TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi reports that Kösem, fearing the inclination to power in her son, 'directed him toward amusement and distracted him with gifts and festivities'. When he took the government effectively into his own hands at about twenty, he was a tall, broad-shouldered, imposing ruler — it is related that he lifted a strong man by the belt and carried him around the Privy Chamber several times. The striking contradiction recorded by the TDV is this: the sultan, said to have loathed tobacco and opium, was excessively fond of drink, yet before campaigns he banned coffee, tobacco and later even drink itself; at the same time he had enough knowledge of music to compose in accordance with the makam tradition.

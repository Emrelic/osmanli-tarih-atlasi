Attachment of the Low Countries to the Spanish crown — the death of Ferdinand and the accession of Charles V

23 January 1516

With the death of Ferdinand II of Aragon on 23 January 1516, his grandson Charles V, who had already ruled the Low Countries (present-day Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg and part of northern France) as Duke of Burgundy since 1506, became the heir to the thrones of Castile and Aragon as well, and was proclaimed King of Spain in Brussels on 14 March 1516. Thus these lands came to be regarded, through personal union, as attached directly to the Spanish crown rather than to the German/Austrian branch of the Habsburg dynasty — with Charles V's election as Holy Roman Emperor in 1519, these two identities (King of Spain and German-Roman emperor) would be united in the same person, while the region's attachment to Spain would last until 1556, when Charles V abdicated and left his inheritance to his son Philip II.

ʻAi Noa: the end of the traditional kapu system in Hawaii

1 November 1819

Six months after the death of Kamehameha I, the new king Liholiho, with the support of his mother Kaʻahumanu, deliberately broke the prohibition on men and women eating together (ʻaikapu). This act, known as 'ʻAi Noa' (free eating), formally ended the traditional kapu religio-legal system; the old temples (heiau) on the islands were soon torn down. It is the greatest religious and political rupture before the arrival of Western missionaries (1820).

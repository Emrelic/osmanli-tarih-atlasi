Safi enters Portuguese influence

1488

Safi, the busiest grain and fishing port of the Atlantic coast, entered Portuguese influence in 1488; the city was for a time governed through local rulers under Portuguese protection and was directly occupied in 1508. Because the sources weight these two stages differently, the starting date varies between 1488 and 1508; both are ends of the same process. Safi remained in Portuguese hands for fifty-three years and was evacuated in 1541 after the fall of Agadir.

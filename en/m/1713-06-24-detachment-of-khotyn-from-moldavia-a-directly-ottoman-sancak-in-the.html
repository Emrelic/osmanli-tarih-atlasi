Detachment of Khotyn from Moldavia: a directly Ottoman sancak in the middle of the voivodeship

24 June 1713

In the Pruth campaign, the going over of the voivode of Moldavia Dimitrie Cantemir to the Russian side showed that the northern border of the voivodeship could no longer be left to the voivode. Thereupon Khotyn was taken from Moldavia after 1711 and brought under direct Ottoman administration; it was first given the status of a nahiye, then of a sancak. After the temporary Russian occupation of 1713, the fortress was repaired and enlarged under the supervision of an Ottoman and French technical mission sent from Istanbul and became the strongest Ottoman fortification north of the Danube. That on the map from this date Khotyn leaves the tone of the vassal voivodeship and returns to the directly Ottoman color, standing like an island alone in the middle of vassal Moldavia, is precisely the counterpart of this administrative separation — it is not a drawing error. The same structure had existed since 1538 at Budjak and since 1484 at Kilia and Akkerman: the voivodeship free in its internal affairs, the frontier fortresses the state's.

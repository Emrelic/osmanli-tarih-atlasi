Second Treaty of Erzurum — the Shatt al-Arab border and the abandonment of the Khuzestan coast

31 May 1847

The Ottoman-Iranian border dispute, which had remained unresolved for more than a century, was settled by the second treaty, signed in Erzurum after long negotiations conducted under British and Russian mediation. The whole of the Shatt al-Arab waterway remained Ottoman; in return, the settlements on the eastern bank of the river — including the port of Muhammara and the island of Abadan opposite it — were left to the Qajars. Thus the coastal strip of Khuzestan, which had been considered under Ottoman administration since the annexation of Basra in 1546, was lost three hundred years later. Mehmed Hurşid Pasha, a member of the boundary commission set up to mark on the ground the line drawn by the treaty, travelled through the region between 1848 and 1852 and wrote the Seyâhatnâme-i Hudûd.

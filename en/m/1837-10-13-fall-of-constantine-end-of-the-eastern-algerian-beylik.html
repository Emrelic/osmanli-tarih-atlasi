Fall of Constantine: end of the eastern Algerian beylik

13 October 1837

After the fall of Algiers in 1830 the eastern beylerbeyilik did not dissolve: the last bey, Ahmed, continued his own administration in Constantine and declared that he was acting in the name of the Ottomans. The first French expedition of 1836 ended in a rout; the second expedition besieged the city on 6 October 1837, the commander Damrémont was killed by cannon fire on 12 October, and on 13 October the walls were breached and the city was taken street by street. Ahmed Bey withdrew to the mountains and surrendered only in 1848. This date is the de facto end of three hundred years of Ottoman-Turkish rule in eastern Algeria.

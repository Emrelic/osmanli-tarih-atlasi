The Coming of the English Fleet before Istanbul (the Duckworth Operation)

20 February 1807

England, wishing to force the Ottoman State to break its tie with France, passed the fleet under Admiral Duckworth through the Dardanelles and anchored it before Istanbul. With the direction also of the French ambassador Sébastiani, the people of Istanbul mobilised, the coastal batteries were reinforced within days, and the fleet was obliged to withdraw before the city could be bombarded; on the return it suffered heavy losses at the Dardanelles. The capital's being threatened so closely by a foreign fleet for the first time placed the question of the Straits at the centre of European diplomacy.

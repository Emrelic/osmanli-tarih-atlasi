Fall of Acre to the Mamluks — liquidation of the Crusaders from the Syrian coast: Sidon and Beirut

18 May 1291

Acre, which had long been the centre of the Crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem, was conquered by the Mamluk Sultan al-Malik al-Ashraf Khalil, and the Crusaders' dominion in Palestine came to an end. The Templar knights who withdrew from Acre took refuge in Sidon; when the Mamluk army approached, they abandoned the sea castle and fled to Cyprus, and the city passed to the Mamluks the same summer. Beirut, too, which had been in Crusader hands since 1197, was taken in 1291 by the governor of Damascus, Sanjar al-Shuja'i. The Crusader presence of two centuries was thus erased from the Syro-Palestinian coast.

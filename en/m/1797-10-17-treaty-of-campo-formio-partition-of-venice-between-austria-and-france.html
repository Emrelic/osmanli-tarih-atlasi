Treaty of Campo Formio — partition of Venice between Austria and France

17 October 1797

The Treaty of Campo Formio, signed on 17 October 1797 between Napoleon Bonaparte and the Austrian representative Count Cobenzl, officially abolished the thousand-year-old Republic of Venice and divided its lands: the area east of the Adige river, Istria and the Dalmatian coast (including Split, Zadar, Šibenik and Kotor) were left to Austria, while the Ionian Islands (Corfu, Cephalonia, Zakynthos, Lefkada, and Venice's bases on the Albanian-Morea coast, including Preveza and Parga) passed to France. Austria also ceded its lands in the Low Countries (present-day Belgium) to France.

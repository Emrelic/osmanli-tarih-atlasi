Malta and Tripoli given to the Knights of Saint John — new base of the order expelled from Rhodes

24 March 1530

The Knights of Saint John (Hospitallers), whom Süleyman the Lawgiver had driven from Rhodes in 928 (1522), after remaining homeless for eight years, were settled on Malta by Charles V, King of Spain and Germany; by the same grant the defence of Tripoli was also given to the order. Malta, with its ideal harbours, became a convenient base for the knights, who were active at sea: they settled first in Birgu, fortified Fort Saint Angelo, and built Fort Saint Michael at the tip of Senglea. This settlement produced a permanent enemy base in the middle of the Mediterranean cutting Ottoman sea routes; the conflicts, which began with Turgut Reis's raid on Gozo in 947 (1540), would continue until the Siege of Malta in 1565.

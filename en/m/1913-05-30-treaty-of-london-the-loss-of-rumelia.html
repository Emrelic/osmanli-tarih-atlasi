Treaty of London — the loss of Rumelia

30 May 1913

By the Treaty of London, which ended the First Balkan War, all lands west of the Midye-Enez line and the Aegean islands were lost; Albania became independent. Of Rumelia, which had for over five hundred years been the empire's demographic, economic and cultural center of gravity, only Eastern Thrace would remain (Edirne was retaken in the Second Balkan War); the loss hardened the war and resettlement policy of the Unionist administration fundamentally. The following places west of the Midye-Enez line were lost: Skopje, Veles, Štip, Strumica, Doiran, Gevgelija, Monastir, Ohrid, Debar (Dibra), Prizren, Pristina, Peć, Novi Pazar, Kosovo Polje; Serres, Drama, Kavala, Petrich, Nevrokop, Xanthi, Komotini, Dedeağaç, Didymoteicho, Çirmen, Feres, Soufli, Orestiada (Kumçiftliği), Praviște (Eleftheroupoli), Stérna, Távri, Umur Fakih (Fakia), Malak Dervent (Lalkovo); in Crete Chania, Candia, Rethymno, Sitia and Sfakia.

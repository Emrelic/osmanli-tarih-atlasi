Treaty of Qasr-i Shirin

17 May 1639

The treaty signed after Murad IV's recapture of Baghdad (1638) ended the Ottoman-Safavid wars of more than a century. Baghdad and Iraq definitively remained with the Ottomans, Revan was left to the Safavids; the line drawn, with minor changes, became the basis of the present Turkey-Iran border and is regarded as one of the longest-lived borders in the world. The treaty left Bedre, Cessân, Mendelî, Derteng and Derne to the Ottomans; the fortress of Zencir on the frontier was to be demolished, the villages to its west being left to the Ottomans, those to its east to Iran. The surroundings of the fortress of Zalim near Şehrizor and Kızılca Kale remained with the Ottomans, Avroman and Mihriban with Iran; the fortresses of Kotur, Mâkû and Mağazberd were to be demolished by both sides.

Georgian King David routs the Muslim army at Didgori; the road to Tbilisi and Ani is opened

12 August 1121

Against the great army obtained by the Georgian King David (David II in TDV; David IV the Builder in Georgian sources) from the Kipchaks, Tughril, ruler of Ganja, sent by Mahmud, the Seljuk Sultan of Iraq, the atabeg Gündoğdu, Dubays ibn Sadaqa, the Artuqid Ilghazi and Dilmaçoğlu Togan Arslan were defeated near Didgori on 12 August 1121. The Georgians then occupied Tbilisi, which had been under Islamic rule for centuries, and made it their capital.

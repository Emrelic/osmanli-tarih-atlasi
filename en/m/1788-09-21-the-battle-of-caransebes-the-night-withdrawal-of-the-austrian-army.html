The battle of Caransebeș — the night withdrawal of the Austrian army

21 September 1788

The Ottoman forces under the command of Kethüdâ Hasan Pasha, the serasker of Vidin, defeated Joseph II's army at Caransebeș also, after Mehadia. In a council in the tent of the grand vizier-commander it was decided to attack the enemy camp; while preparations continued during the night, the Austrian troops were seen to have abandoned their positions and withdrawn to the town of Caransebeș. The pursuing Ottoman forces pinned them down in the town; in the fire that broke out many soldiers were killed, the survivors took refuge at Lugoj, seven hours away, and more than a thousand people were taken prisoner. The Austrian army's own campaign diary records the same night as a panic and friendly fire; for a comparison of the two sides' accounts, further reading: karsi-karansebes-1788. The city did not come into Ottoman possession: it was taken, burned and abandoned on 10 October 1788 — for this reason no change of frontier appears on the map.

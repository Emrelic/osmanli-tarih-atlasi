Construction of Yenikale — the locking of the Strait of Kerch

17 May 1703

After the cession of Azak to Russia in 1700, the only gate by which the Black Sea opens to the Sea of Azov became a strategic frontier line. The Ottoman Empire had a new fortification built at the locality of Akıntı Burnu, ten kilometres northeast of the city of Kerch, and brought the passage of the strait within cannon range; the fortress was given the name Yenikale, which is simply what it means. For seventy years the fortress remained the eastern lock of Crimea, and with Kerch it was ceded to Russia by the nineteenth article of the Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca in 1774.

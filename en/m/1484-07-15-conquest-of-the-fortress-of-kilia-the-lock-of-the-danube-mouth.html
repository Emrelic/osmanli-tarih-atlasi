Conquest of the Fortress of Kilia: the lock of the Danube mouth

15 July 1484

Moldavia had been since September 1455 a vassal voivodeship paying two thousand gold pieces annually as tribute, but its two ports opening to the Black Sea were still in the voivode's hands and the trade coming down the Danube passed through them. Bayezid II made the first great campaign of his reign against these two ports. The commander of the Fortress of Kilia, shelled night and day for ten days, was forced to surrender. The fortress was taken under direct Ottoman administration and made a sancak beylik; in the second half of the sixteenth century, because of the Cossack danger, it was made a kaza and attached to the sancak of Akkerman, itself attached to the beylerbeyilik of Rumelia. From here on Moldavia appears on the map in two tiers: inland the voivodeship stands as a light-toned vassal territory, while the two ports on the coast are a dark-toned directly Ottoman sancak. The TDV entry on Boğdan (Moldavia) calls these two 'the keys and gates of Moldavia' and writes that the region was definitively attached to the Ottomans under Bayezid II.

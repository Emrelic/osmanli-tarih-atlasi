Seti I campaigned from southern Canaan to the Phoenician coast: Egypt again became dominant over Canaan and the Lebanese coast, and took Kadesh

1293 BC

According to the Karnak reliefs, Seti I's campaign of year 1 first subdued the Shasu bedouin, then going north through Palestine subdued Pehel and Beth-Shean, Yenoam, Tyre and the princes of Lebanon. The capture of Kadesh is also shown in the upper register of the reliefs. Breasted notes that the lasting northern frontier did not extend beyond the east-west line along the Phoenician coast and that Hittite power was not broken; this is the background to Ramesses II's battle of Kadesh.

The First Canal Operation: The Attack on Suez across the Sinai Desert

14 January 1915

Cemal Pasha, the commander of the Fourth Army, mounted an attack across the Sinai desert with the aim of freeing Egypt from the British occupation and closing the Suez Canal to traffic. Although the Ottoman units reached the eastern bank of the canal and some managed to cross, they withdrew in the face of British superiority and the failure of the expected Egyptian popular uprising to materialise. The operation did not reach its objective, but by obliging England to keep a large force in Egypt it created an indirect strategic effect.

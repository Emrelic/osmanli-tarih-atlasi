The Treaty of Istanbul with Venice — The Recognition of Cyprus

7 March 1573

Venice, left alone by the dissolution of the Holy League, made a separate peace with the Ottomans, officially accepted that Cyprus belonged to the Ottomans, and consented to pay a war indemnity. The treaty made clear that the victory of Lepanto had brought the Christian world no political gain. From then on Venice would avoid entering into direct war with the Ottomans for seventy years.

The Treaty of Kerden — The End of the Ottoman-Iranian Wars

4 September 1746

By the treaty signed after the last war, lasting four years, the Qasr-i Shirin frontier of 1639 was renewed exactly; neither side obtained a territorial gain. Nadir Shah's insistent demand that Jafarism be recognised as a fifth school of law went unanswered; in return it was decided that Iranian pilgrims would perform the pilgrimage freely and that envoys would be exchanged. The Ottoman-Iranian wars, which had lasted more than two centuries, were thus effectively closed; this frontier has been largely preserved to the present day.

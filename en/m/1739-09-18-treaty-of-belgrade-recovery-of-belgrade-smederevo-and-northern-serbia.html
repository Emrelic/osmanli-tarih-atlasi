Treaty of Belgrade — recovery of Belgrade, Smederevo and northern Serbia

18 September 1739

At the end of the war (1736–1739) conducted on two fronts against Austria and Russia, Belgrade and Northern Serbia were recovered; Russia agreed to demilitarize Azov and not to keep a fleet on the Black Sea. The treaty, regarded as the last great success of 18th-century Ottoman diplomacy and army, gave the empire a quarter-century period of peace. Other settlements returning from Austria to the Ottomans on the same day: Hisarcık (Grocka) (the date of Belgrade was taken as the basis; TDV Belgrad), Craiova, Râmnicu Vâlcea and Turnu Severin (the return of Lesser Wallachia to the Ottomans, to Wallachian vassalage). Bosanski Brod and Bosanska Dubica on the Sava strip, however, return to the Ottomans on the map on 28 September 1739 (TDV Bosna-Hersek; TDV I. Mahmud: 28 September 1739). Târgu Jiu in Lesser Wallachia also returns to the Ottomans on the same day.

Treaty of Neuilly — Bulgaria lost Western Thrace and the Aegean coast

27 November 1919

With the war ending in defeat, Tsar Ferdinand had been forced to ask for an armistice and abdicate in favour of his son Boris (29 September 1918). Under the Treaty of Neuilly, which ended the war, Bulgaria suffered a strategic loss of territory in favour of the Serbs, lost the whole of Western Thrace, had to cede its Aegean Sea coast to Greece, and confirmed the provisions of the Treaty of Bucharest that gave Southern Dobruja to Romania. On the map, the counterpart of this transfer is not on the day of the treaty but in May 1920, when the administration actually changed hands: Dedeağaç, Sofulu, Orestiada and Çirmen pass to Greece on 14 May, and Gümülcine, İskeçe, Dimetoka and Ferecik on 27 May.

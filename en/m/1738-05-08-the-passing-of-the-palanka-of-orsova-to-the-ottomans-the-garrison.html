The passing of the palanka of Orsova to the Ottomans — the garrison commander's withdrawal to Ada Kale

8 May 1738

At the opening of the campaign of 1738, Hacı Mehmed Pasha, the commandant of Vidin, attacked the Erzsébet redoubt on the Danube. On the afternoon of 8 May a sipahi detachment of 4,000–5,000 men marched from the left bank of the Danube to Orsova and attacked the palanka of Orsova; the Austrian commander, after losing about a hundred men, withdrew to New Orsova, that is, to the island fortress of Ada Kale. After the Mehadia engagements in July, Ada Kale too passed into Ottoman hands in August 1738; the Treaty of Belgrade of 1739 recognized the situation.

Withdrawal from Wallachia and the Giurgiu ambush: failure of the suppression campaign

1 October 1595

Upon the counteroffensive of Michael the Brave, who had joined forces with the Prince of Transylvania, Sinan Pasha evacuated Târgoviște and Bucharest and withdrew to the Danube. The costliest moment of the withdrawal came at the crossing: in October 1595 the akıncıs pursuing the army returning from Wallachia were ambushed by Michael at Giurgiu. The campaign thus ended without reaching its goal of bringing Wallachia under direct administration, and the voivodeship remained effectively lost. Ottoman authority in this triangle could be repaired only over the years and piece by piece; Transylvania's return to obedience would wait for the Bocskai uprising in 1604, and the complete closing of the front for the Treaty of Zsitvatorok in 1606.

End of the Venetian Republic — the Great Council dissolved itself

12 May 1797

When Napoleon's Italian campaign army occupied the Venetian mainland and issued an ultimatum to the republic, the Great Council (Maggior Consiglio) voted on 12 May 1797, by 512 to 20, to dissolve itself; the last doge, Ludovico Manin, resigned, and the aristocratic government of the Venetian Republic, which had existed without interruption for more than 1,100 years, came to an end. Its territories were handed first to a provisional democratic government, and then formally ceded to Austria by the Treaty of Campo Formio in October 1797.

Fall of the Golden Horde Khanate and the rise of Crimea

1 March 1502

Its last ruler, Sheikh Ahmed Khan (1481-1502), could not save his country from the crisis into which it had fallen, and the Golden Horde Khanate passed into history in 1502 through the coup of the Crimean khan Mengli Giray. As a result of struggles for the throne and fragmentation, the khanates of Crimea, Kazan, Nogai, Astrakhan and Sibir emerged on the former Golden Horde lands. This triumph of Crimea, under Ottoman protection, made Ottoman influence north of the Black Sea unquestionable, while the vacuum that arose in the region would be filled by Moscow in the following century.

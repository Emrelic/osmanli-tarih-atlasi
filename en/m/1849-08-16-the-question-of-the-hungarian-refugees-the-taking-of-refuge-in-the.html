The Question of the Hungarian Refugees: The Taking of Refuge in the Ottoman Lands by Kossuth and His Companions

16 August 1849

When the Hungarian revolution of 1848-1849 was suppressed by Russian and Austrian intervention, Lajos Kossuth and thousands of Hungarian and Polish refugees took refuge in Ottoman territory. Austria and Russia sternly demanded the extradition of the refugees; the Sublime Porte, with the support of England and France, refused extradition and settled the group at Kütahya. Kossuth's circle stayed there for about eighteen months, and hundreds of refugees became Muslims and entered Ottoman service. The event was regarded as one of the rare diplomatic victories that raised the standing of the Tanzimat Ottoman Empire in European public opinion.

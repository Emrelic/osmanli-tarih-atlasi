The loss of Yemen — the domination of the Qasimi imams

1635

Yemen had been an Ottoman province since 1538; however, the resistance of the Zaydi imams never ceased. The uprising begun by Imam al-Qasim spread across the whole country under his son al-Mu'ayyad Muhammad, and the Ottoman garrisons were besieged one after another. In the years when Murad IV was focused on Baghdad and Revan, no reinforcements could be sent; Taiz had been lost in 1629, while Mocha had been evacuated on 22 October 1635, in the final phase of the same collapse. In 1635 the last troops in Sanaa and Zabid also evacuated Yemen. Thus the eastern coast of the Red Sea remained outside the empire for two hundred and thirty-seven years, and could be recovered only in 1872. According to the TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, Lahij (al-Hawta) was in Ottoman hands until 1635; from that date the Zaydi imams became influential.

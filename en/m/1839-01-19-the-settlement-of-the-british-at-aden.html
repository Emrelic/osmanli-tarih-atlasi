The Settlement of the British at Aden

19 January 1839

England, relying on the trade and friendship agreement it had signed in 1802 with the sultan of Lahej, Ahmed b. Abd al-Karim, landed troops at the port of Aden and took the city under its control. This port, holding the mouth of the Red Sea, turned into a key point of British imperial strategy as the coaling base on the route to India. Although the Ottoman State's claim over Yemen continued on paper, its de facto dominion on the southern coast never returned after this date.

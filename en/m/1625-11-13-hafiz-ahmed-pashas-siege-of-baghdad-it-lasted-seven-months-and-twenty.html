Hafız Ahmed Pasha's siege of Baghdad — it lasted seven months and twenty days and ended without result

13 November 1625

The Ottomans, who had lost Baghdad in 1623, marched under the grand vizier and commander Hafız Ahmed Pasha to retake the city. When the proposal, at the council assembled at Kirkuk, to hold the passes first was not accepted, the army besieged Baghdad directly. The pasha himself entered the trenches; although the city was entered through a mine exploded on the seventy-second day, the soldiers were repulsed. When Shah Abbas came to its aid with 30,000 men in March 1626, the army was caught between two fires; heavy losses were suffered in the shah's attack of 7 June. When provisions ran out and the heat and fever were wearing down the soldiers, the siege was lifted and the army withdrew to Diyarbekir.

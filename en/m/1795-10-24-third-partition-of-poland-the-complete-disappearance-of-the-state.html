Third Partition of Poland — the complete disappearance of the state

24 October 1795

By a three-power convention they signed at St. Petersburg on 24 October 1795, Russia, Prussia and Austria completely partitioned the remaining territory of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth: Russia took Lithuania (including Vilnius and Kaunas) and the remainder of Belarus, Prussia annexed central Poland including Warsaw, and Austria annexed the southern region including Kraków. The three states also guaranteed one another's new territories militarily and took measures against the possibility of Poland's re-establishment. This was the complete disappearance of the Polish-Lithuanian state as an independent country; it would not be re-established until the twentieth century.

Treaty of Gallipoli: the Black Sea coast as far as Mesembria ceded to Byzantium

1 February 1403

Emir Süleyman, who wished to hold on in Rumelia after the Ankara disaster, signed a treaty with the Byzantine emperor Manuel at Gallipoli. He ceded to Byzantium Kartal, Pendik and Gebze together with certain islands, Thessalonica and Thessaly in Rumelia, and the coastal strip on the Black Sea extending as far as Mesembria; the tribute that Byzantium paid to the Ottomans was also abolished. Agathopolis, İğneada and Rezve on the Istranca coast thus remained under Byzantine administration for twenty-one years; Murad II recovered most of these places with the peace of 1424.

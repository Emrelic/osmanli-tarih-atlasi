The Taking of Sana'a: The Re-establishment of Ottoman Authority in Yemen

1 April 1872

When the strategic value of the Red Sea increased with the opening of the Suez Canal, the Sublime Porte sent the reserve army (fırka-i ihtiyâtiyye) to Yemen under the command of Ahmed Muhtar Pasha. With the taking of Sana'a, state authority was re-established in inner Yemen, which had been effectively lost for two centuries, and the region was made a vilayet. However, the struggle with the Zaydi imams never ended; the rebellions of 1891, 1898 and 1904 formed the Ottomans' most costly and longest-lasting internal front. Other places that joined on the same date: Sana'a, Taiz.

The Istanbul Mukāsemenâme — The Partition of Iran with Russia

24 June 1724

A partition treaty was signed through the mediation of the French ambassador to prevent the Ottoman Empire and Russia from coming face to face over the collapsing Safavid realm. The western and southern shores of the Caspian Sea were left to Russia; Georgia, Shirvan, Azerbaijan and the cities of western Iran were left to the Ottoman State. This partition, which looked like a great gain on paper, would soon become void in practice with the rise of Nader Shah. According to the treaty, the fertile lands of Shirvan such as Shabran and Mushkur were left on the Russian side (Çaycıoğlu, DergiPark).

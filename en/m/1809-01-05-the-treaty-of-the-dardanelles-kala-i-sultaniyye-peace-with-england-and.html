The Treaty of the Dardanelles (Kal'a-i Sultâniyye) — Peace with England and the Rule of the Straits

5 January 1809

By the treaty signed with England after the failure of the Duckworth operation, the war between the two states ended and the old privileges of English merchants were restored. The provision in the text called the 'ancient rule' recorded that in peacetime no foreign warship could pass through the Straits. This principle became the legal basis of all the international arrangements that would shape the regime of the Straits throughout the nineteenth century.

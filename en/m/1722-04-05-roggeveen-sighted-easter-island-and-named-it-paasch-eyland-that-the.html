Roggeveen sighted Easter Island and named it Paasch Eyland; that the island was inhabited was inferred the next day from smoke

5 April 1722

According to the official logbook, Jacob Roggeveen's expedition sighted a low, flat island on 5 April 1722 and, since it was Easter day, named it Paasch Eyland. On 6 April smoke was seen rising from various places and it was concluded that people lived on the island; Behrens's narrative places the discovery on 6 April.

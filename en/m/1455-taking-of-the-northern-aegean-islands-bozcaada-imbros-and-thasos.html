Taking of the northern Aegean islands: Bozcaada, Imbros and Thasos

1455

Immediately after the conquest of Constantinople, Mehmed the Conqueror turned to the islands at the Aegean mouth of the Dardanelles in order to secure the new capital's sea route; Tenedos (Bozcaada) and Imbros (İmroz) were taken from Byzantium, and Thasos from the Gattilusio family of Genoese origin. These islands, which lock both sides of the Straits, became thereafter the first link in Istanbul's provisioning and defence line. With the interventions of the papal and Venetian fleets dominion went back and forth for a time and was settled only in 1459.

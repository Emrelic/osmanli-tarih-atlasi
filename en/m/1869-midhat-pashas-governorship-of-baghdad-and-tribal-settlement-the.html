Midhat Pasha's governorship of Baghdad and tribal settlement: the founding of Ramadi and Nasiriyya

1869

Removed from the presidency of the Council of State and sent away from Istanbul, Midhat Pasha governed the province of Baghdad, which also included Mosul and Basra, with broad powers between 1869 and 1872, holding the command of the Sixth Army as well. Applying the Land Code and the new provincial law here, he distributed lands by title deed and bound the nomadic tribes to a settled order and to state authority. As a product of this settlement policy, in 1869 Ramadi was founded on the Euphrates for the Dulaym tribe, and in the south Nasiriyya was founded by the hand of Nasir Pasha al-Sa'dun, the mutasarrif of the Muntafiq; both arose at points where there had been no settlement until then and entered the map for the first time on this date.

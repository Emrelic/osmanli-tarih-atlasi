Uprising of the three voivodeships begins: Transylvania joins the Holy League, pro-Ottoman nobles arrested

28 August 1594

In the second year of the Long War, the Ottoman vassal belt in Central Europe suddenly unraveled. Moldavia and Transylvania joined the Holy League, founded under the patronage of Pope Clement VIII, at the end of summer: the voivode of Moldavia Aron Vodă formalized by a document on 16 August 1594 his joining the Christian side, and the Prince of Transylvania Zsigmond Báthory on 28 August 1594 broke the opposition within by purging the pro-Ottoman nobles who opposed the war. The voivode of Wallachia Michael the Brave joined the alliance in the autumn of the same year. Thus the three vassal principalities of the Ottomans north of the Danube — three voivodeships that had paid tribute for a hundred and fifty years and whose voivodes were confirmed by Istanbul — went over to the other side all at once in the same autumn. The decision was formalized by the treaty signed at Prague on 28 January 1595. This was the severest shock suffered by the Ottoman system of indirect rule in the northern Balkans; the uprising would be suppressed but the loyalty of the three principalities would never again be as firm as it had been between 1526 and 1593.

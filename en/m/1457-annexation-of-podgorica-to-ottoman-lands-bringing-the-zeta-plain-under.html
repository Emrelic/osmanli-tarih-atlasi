Annexation of Podgorica to Ottoman lands: bringing the Zeta plain under control

1457

The town, situated twenty kilometers north of Lake Shkodër on the plain where the waters of the Morača and Ribnica meet, is mentioned in the sources for the first time under the name Ribnica in 1216 and as Podgorica in 1326. It was annexed to Ottoman lands in 1457, and control of the Zeta plain thus passed out of the hands of the Balšić-Crnojević dynasties. Mehmed the Conqueror had a fortress and a mosque built between 1474 and 1478 on the steep rocks at the confluence of the two rivers; after the conquest of Shkodër in 1479 the region was attached to the newly established sancak of Shkodër. Podgorica, a Christian town of forty households in the survey of 1485, reached 800 people and a thirty-two-man garrison under a dizdar in the fortress in 1529-30, and 322 households in 1582. In 1662 Evliya Çelebi counted three hundred houses, storehouses, arsenals and seven hundred guards in the Fortress of the Conqueror. These records also show the difference between the plain and the mountains of Zeta: the plain is a counted, taxed and garrisoned Ottoman kaza — yet in the eighteenth century the city would have to be fortified with six bastions and a three-gated wall to prevent raids by the tribes in the Montenegrin mountains. Podgorica passed to Montenegro at the end of the war of 1876-1878 and remained there by the Treaty of Berlin.

Selim III and the Nizâm-ı Cedîd

7 April 1789

Selim III, who came to the throne in the middle of the war, seeing that the defeats were structural, began a comprehensive reform programme under the name 'Nizâm-ı Cedîd' (New Order): a new army drilled in the European manner, a separate treasury financing it (Îrâd-ı Cedîd), the strengthening of the engineering schools, and the first permanent embassies in European capitals. The programme is regarded as the beginning of the modern Turkish reform tradition; however, through the resistance of the corps and the provincial notables (âyan) it would be overthrown in 1807.

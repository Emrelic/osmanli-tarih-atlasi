The First Treaty with the United States of America

7 May 1830

By the treaty of commerce and navigation signed after years of contacts, the United States obtained in the Ottoman lands the status of most favoured nation and the right of commercial passage through the Straits. A separate secret article envisaged that the Ottomans, whenever they wished, would have ships built in American shipyards and buy ready-made ships and timber; the United States Senate ratified the treaty but rejected this secret article by 27 votes to 18. The ship route was nonetheless not closed: Henry Eckford sold to the Ottomans in 1831 the ship he had brought, and American craftsmen working at Aynalıkavak gave the fleet new types of ships. This treaty became the document of the first comprehensive relationship the Ottoman State established with a state outside Europe.

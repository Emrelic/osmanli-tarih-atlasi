Byzantium made tributary — siege of İzmit and treaty

1 August 1333

TDV, Orhan entry: in the summer of 733 (1333) Orhan besieged İzmit with a large army and with mangonels, and the emperor consented to a treaty. In return for his giving up the siege, Byzantium agreed to pay Orhan 12,000 gold pieces (hyperpyra) a year; 'thus in the eyes of the Ottoman emir Byzantium fell to the condition of a tribute-paying country'. In return Orhan would be a friend of the emperor and would not attack the cities subject to Byzantium. A frontier beylik's imposing tribute on the Eastern Roman Empire was a proclamation of how far the balance of power had turned.

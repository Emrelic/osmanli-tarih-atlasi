Bucharest uprising and the attack on the Danube fortresses

1 November 1594

The decision of the Holy League was carried out in blood a month later: Michael the Brave put the Ottoman garrison in Bucharest and the Levantine creditors in the city to the sword. The uprising spread to the Danube line the same winter; Wallachian forces attacked Giurgiu, Brăila, Hârșova and Silistra, while on the Moldavian side Aron Vodă made the same assault on the Ottoman guards at Bender. The choice of the places struck is no coincidence: all of these were not voivodeship land but fortresses and kazas along the Danube under direct Ottoman administration — the kaza of Giurgiu was attached to the sancak beylik of Nikopol, Brăila had been directly under the state since 1538, and Bender had likewise been attached to the sancak of Budjak since 1538. That is, the revolt had struck not at the vassal interior but at the directly Ottoman shell surrounding it. Without piercing this shell, the Transylvania-Wallachia-Moldavia triangle could not be joined to the Habsburg front.

Fall of Granada — end of the last Muslim state on the Iberian Peninsula

2 January 1492

Abu Abdallah (Boabdil), the last ruler of the Nasrid Emirate of Granada, which had been weakened by a gradual siege lasting ten years and by internal divisions, handed the keys of the city to the Catholic Monarchs Ferdinand and Isabella on 2 January 1492, in accordance with the generous terms of surrender (the Capitulations of Granada) signed in November 1491; after Boabdil left the Alhambra, the last Muslim state on the Iberian Peninsula came to an end and the Reconquista was completed. The expulsion of the Jews (March) and Christopher Columbus's voyage (August) would follow within the same year.

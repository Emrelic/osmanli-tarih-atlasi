Incorporation of Jaghbub, the desert center of the Sanusiyya, into the Ottoman order: Abdulmejid's decree of exemption

1856

After returning to Tripolitania following many years in Mecca, Muhammad b. Ali al-Sanusi settled in the oasis of Jaghbub on the Egyptian border and had a large zawiya built on the commanding rocky height; the place, consisting of a few huts, soon turned into a town. In 1856, by decree of Sultan Abdulmejid, the properties belonging to the members of the order were exempted from taxation and the disciples were given the authority to collect zakat. This decree made the oasis, which lay on the caravan route linking West Africa and the Sudan to Cairo, part of the Ottoman fiscal and administrative order and strengthened Jaghbub as a trade center. The local population's allegiance to the caliph is also confirmed by the travel account of Colonel Sadiq al-Mu'ayyad, who visited the place in 1884 and 1893.

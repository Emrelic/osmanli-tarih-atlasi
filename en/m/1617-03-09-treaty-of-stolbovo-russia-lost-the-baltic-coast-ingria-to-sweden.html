Treaty of Stolbovo — Russia lost the Baltic coast (Ingria) to Sweden

9 March 1617

The Treaty of Stolbovo, which ended the Ingrian War, was signed on 27 February 1617 in the village of Stolbovo, south of Lake Ladoga; while Russia recovered Novgorod and its other northern lands, Sweden kept Karelia and Ingria (between Estonia and Finland, including the fortresses of Ivangorod, Yam, Koporye and Oreshek), and Russia's direct access to the Baltic coast was cut off. This severance would last until 1703, when Peter the Great founded St. Petersburg in the same region.

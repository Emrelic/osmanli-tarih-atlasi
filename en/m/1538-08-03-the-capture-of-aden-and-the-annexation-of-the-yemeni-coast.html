The Capture of Aden and the Annexation of the Yemeni Coast

3 August 1538

The fleet that left Suez for the Indian campaign turned first to the Yemeni coast, because the ocean could not be entered without securing the Red Sea. Hadım Süleyman Pasha entered the port of Aden on 3 August 1538; the Tahirid ruler Amir b. Dawud, who inclined toward the Portuguese, was brought to the captain's gate and executed, and a guard of 500 men was left in the city. During the same campaign Mocha and the islands of Kamaran and Farasan, which command the strait, were also taken over, and the foundation of the beylerbeyilik of Yemen was laid. Thus the Ottoman State for the first time came to possess the whole of the northern shore of Bab al-Mandab; Sana'a and the inner regions would have to wait nine more years.

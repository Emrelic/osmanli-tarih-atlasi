The dismissal and execution of Grand Vizier Halil Hamid Pasha

27 April 1785

Halil Hamid Pasha, the reformist grand vizier with whose services Abdülhamid I had on various occasions expressed his satisfaction, was suddenly dismissed toward the morning of 31 March 1785 and exiled to Gallipoli, and all his property was confiscated. He was first given the province of Habesh (Abyssinia), the sancak of Jeddah and the şeyhülharemlik of Mecca; while spending April 1785 on Tenedos making preparations for the journey, he was also dismissed from this governorship, and on the death decree that was issued he was killed by Karakethüdâzâde Ali, one of the men of Cezayirli Gazi Hasan Pasha. His severed head was sent to Istanbul and displayed at the Ortakapı. According to the TDV, although this sudden fall has been interpreted with the accusation of a plot to depose the sultan and put Prince Selim in his place, the real causes must have been the outrage at his silence over the annexation of Crimea, the hostility of the circles injured by his reforms, and the opposition of Gazi Hasan Pasha.

Admission to the Ottoman lands of the Sephardic Jews expelled from Spain

1 August 1492

After the fall of Granada, some of the Jews fleeing the persecution of the Inquisition in Spain took refuge in Ottoman lands and were settled in various cities. Under Ottoman rule these Sephardic communities gained a freedom they had not known until then: to settle where they wished, to practise their faith, to open educational institutions and to build new synagogues. This migration, which transformed the economic life of Thessalonica and Istanbul with the printing, medical and financial knowledge they brought with them, became the most discussed example in Europe of the Ottoman order of tolerance.

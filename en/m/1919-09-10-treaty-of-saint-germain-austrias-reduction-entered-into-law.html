Treaty of Saint-Germain — Austria's reduction entered into law

10 September 1919

After the war Austria signed the Treaty of Saint-Germain with the Allies. According to the treaty Austria recognized the independence of Hungary, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia; it agreed not to unite with Germany without the consent of the League of Nations, to abolish compulsory military service and to limit the army to 30,000. It is also the document by which Czechoslovakia gained its place in international law. The transfer on the map had occurred a year earlier; this treaty registered it. By this treaty Lviv, Jablunkov, Karviná and the Dalmatian coast and islands (Krk, Cres, Lošinj, Rab, Pag, Hvar, Korčula, Vis, Zadar, Šibenik, Knin) passed from Austria to the disposal of the Principal Allied Powers; Uzhhorod and Mukachevo (Carpathian Ruthenia) to Czechoslovakia.

Death of Skanderbeg — extinction of Albanian resistance

17 January 1468

Skanderbeg, who, although raised at the Ottoman court, rebelled in 1444 after the Izladi rout and resisted in Albania for more than a quarter century, died at Lezhë (Leş), where he had taken refuge. This resistance, which in the reigns of Murad II and the Conqueror repeatedly occupied Ottoman armies, lost its central leadership with his death. With the collapse of the Albanian front, nourished by the papacy and Venice, the Ottomans took Akçahisar and Shkodër within ten years and definitively annexed the region to their lands.

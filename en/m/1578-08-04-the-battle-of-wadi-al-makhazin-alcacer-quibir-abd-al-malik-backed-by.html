The Battle of Wadi al-Makhazin (Alcácer Quibir) — Abd al-Malik, Backed by the Ottomans, Annihilated the Portuguese Army

4 August 1578

Abd al-Malik, who had taken the throne of Morocco with Ottoman support, annihilated the great army of King Sebastian of Portugal at Wadi al-Makhazin; because three rulers died in the battle at once, it is called the 'Battle of the Three Kings' in Western sources. Portugal's military power collapsed, and the country soon passed into the hands of Spain. Abd al-Malik also died in the battle; his brother Ahmad al-Mansur, who succeeded him, announced the victory to Istanbul with a delegation. The relationship between the two states remained at the level of an exchange of envoys and gifts, not tribute or vassalage, and soon became strained over the question of gifts.

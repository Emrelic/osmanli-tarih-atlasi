Closing of the Lower Danube: annexation of Giurgiu, Turnu and Orșova

1420

After the end of the Interregnum, Çelebi Mehmed's move to put the Danube line in order bore fruit in 1420. The Fortress of Giurgiu, which the Voivode of Wallachia Mircea the Elder had built on his own land, covering the cost through the sale of salt, passed this year into Ottoman hands (Dobruja had been retaken a year earlier, in the spring of 1419 — TDV silistre), and Turnu (Kule/Holovnik) on the right bank of the Danube and Orșova — later called Fethülislam — were annexed. Thus both banks of the river were brought under control at once. The importance of Giurgiu comes from its geography: it is an Ottoman fortress established on the left, that is the Wallachian, bank of the Danube, and together with Ruse on the opposite bank holds the two ends of the same crossing. Ottoman documents state this duality explicitly — for Ruse it is said 'Yergöğü this bank,' for Giurgiu 'Yergöğü the far bank.' The fortress would be retaken by the Wallachians in 1427, would come under Ottoman control again in 1449, and the kaza of Giurgiu, attached to the sancak of Nikopol, would become an island directly administered within the voivodeship. Here begins the answer to why the Danube border of Wallachia is dark-colored on the map.

Conquest of Constantinople

29 May 1453

After a siege of fifty-four days (6 April–29 May 1453), on the morning of 29 May the Ottoman army crossed the walls and entered the city; the last emperor, Constantine XI, died in the fighting and the 1,100-year-old Eastern Roman Empire passed into history. The young sultan took the title 'the Conqueror' (Fatih), made the city his capital and converted Hagia Sophia into a mosque; the city was soon revived by resettlement and construction. According to the TDV, Silivri and Vize were definitively conquered only in 1453; according to the TDV entry on Lemnos, the island came under Ottoman administration immediately after the conquest and was left to the Gattilusio family on condition of tribute. In the atlas, Silivri, Vize and Lemnos are recorded as passing from Byzantium to the Ottomans from this day (Lemnos as a tributary). The casting of giant cannon, the overland transport of ships and the siege arrangement before the walls are regarded as the peak of the military engineering of the period. In European historiography the conquest is counted among the symbolic dates of the end of the Middle Ages.

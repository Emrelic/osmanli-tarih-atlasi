Reduction of Norway to a province bound to the Danish crown

1537

In his 1536 coronation charter (håndfæstning), King Christian III of Denmark declared that Norway would no longer be regarded as a separate kingdom but would be governed as a province of Denmark, and that the Norwegian Council of the Realm (riksråd) was abolished; this decision became effective in 1537 with the extension of the Danish Church Ordinance to Norway, and the resistance of the Scoto-Norwegian bishop Olav Engelbriktsson was broken. Although Norway retained its own laws and institutions in practice for some time longer, its political status was now that of a province attached to the Danish crown rather than an independent kingdom — a situation that would last until 1814.

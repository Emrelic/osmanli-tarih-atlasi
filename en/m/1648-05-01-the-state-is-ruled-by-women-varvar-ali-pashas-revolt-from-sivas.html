'The state is ruled by women' — Varvar Ali Pasha's revolt from Sivas

1 May 1648

Varvar Ali Pasha, beylerbeyi of Sivas, refused to pay the 30,000 kuruş that Istanbul demanded under the name of 'bayramiye', saying 'Sivas's annual revenue would not cover this sum'; according to the TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, the justification of his revolt was the claim that, because of the sultan's indisposition, state affairs had passed into the hands of women and that this created instability through frequent changes of governors. Although he first won military success in May 1648, he was defeated by İpşir Mustafa Pasha, executed on 20 May 1648 and his head sent to Istanbul.

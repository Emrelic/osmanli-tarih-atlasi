The first commercial ahidname with England — capitulations granted to the English

1580

William Harborne, the English representative sent to Istanbul in October 1578, obtained an ahidname dated 1580 from Murad III with the support of Sokollu Mehmed Pasha and Hoca Sadeddin Efendi. With this ahidname, English merchants gained the right to benefit to the same degree from the commercial privileges (capitulations) previously granted to the French and the Venetians. Letters were exchanged between Queen Elizabeth I and Murad III, and an official relationship was also established between the two states. Immediately after these privileges, the Levant Company was founded in London in 1581; the English conducted their trade in the region through this company until 1825.

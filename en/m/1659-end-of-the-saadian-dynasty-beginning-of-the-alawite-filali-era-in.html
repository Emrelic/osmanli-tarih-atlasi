End of the Saadian dynasty: beginning of the Alawite (Filali) era in Morocco

1659

The TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi defines the Saadians as "an Islamic dynasty that ruled in Morocco (1511-1659)." The dynasty, which in 1549 ended Wattasid rule and united all of Morocco, which in 1578 annihilated the Portuguese army at Wadi al-Makhazin, and which was never at any time attached to the Ottomans, came to an end in 1659 after long struggles for the throne. It was replaced by the Alawite (Filali) sharifs from Tafilalt; Mulay Rashid entered the city of Fez in 1666 and consolidated the new dynasty, and his brother Mulay Ismail cleared the European positions on the Atlantic coast.

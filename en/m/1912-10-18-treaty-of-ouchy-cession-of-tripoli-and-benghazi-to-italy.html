Treaty of Ouchy: cession of Tripoli and Benghazi to Italy

18 October 1912

The Sublime Porte, unable to risk a two-front conflict in the face of the war preparations of the Balkan states, signed a secret peace with Italy in the Swiss town of Ouchy and gave up Tripoli and Benghazi. The Ottoman State thus lost its last territory in Africa; although the return of Rhodes and the Twelve Islands occupied by Italy was promised in return, this promise was never fulfilled. When the treaty was signed the Balkan War had long since begun. With this event Murzuk (Fezzan), Ghat, Sabha, Ubari, Traghan, Zawila and al-Qatrun (Fezzan (sanjak of the vilayet of Tripoli)) passed to Italian administration.
